SEO playbook: applied to this food blog

How people will find Zaika Worlds

Good food alone does not bring visitors. Search does. This section covers all 15 parts of the SEO plan in English, on-page, technical, keywords, intent, analytics, mobile, Core Web Vitals, crawling, indexing, content, links, modern AI search, and the action plan, each one written for this food blog, with a real recipe as the example. Nothing here is theory only: the last section says what to do, in what order.

1. On-Page SEO & Keyword Research: done on every recipe page

On-page SEO is everything on the page itself that helps Google understand it, and helps a person decide to click it. Every recipe on the real site will follow the same pattern, shown here with Chicken Biryani.

What Google would show

zaikaworlds.com/chicken-biryani-recipe/ Clean URL ✓
Chicken Biryani Recipe: Easy Karachi Style | Zaika Worlds

Learn Karachi-style chicken biryani at home: marinated chicken, fragrant basmati rice and dum cooking. Full ingredients, easy steps and chef tips.

Title tag: 57 characters, under the 60-character limit, so it is not cut off in search. Meta description: 146 characters, a short summary under the title that says what the page gives the reader. Both are written for people first, with the main keyword in them naturally.

H1: ONE PER PAGE
Chicken Biryani Recipe
H2
Ingredients · Method · Chef’s Tip
H3
Marinate the Chicken · Layer the Rice · Cook on Dum

One H1 only. H2s break the page into its main parts. H3s break those parts into steps. A reader, and Google, can scan the whole page in seconds.

The on-page checklist for every recipe

  • Title tag under 60 characters: main keyword near the start, one clear promise, site name at the end.
  • Meta description: what the dish is, what the page contains, and why this version is worth opening.
  • Headings in order: one H1, then H2, then H3. Never skip levels, and never use bold text as a fake heading.
  • Clean, short URL: /chicken-biryani-recipe/, not a long link full of numbers, dates and symbols.
  • Content quality: unique, long enough to actually cook from, and useful, intro, ingredients with quantities, numbered steps, time, servings and a tip. No copy-paste from another site.
  • Image alt text: Google cannot see a photo, so the text behind it must say what it shows. See the example below.
  • Internal linking: every recipe links to related recipes and categories, so readers stay and Google can follow the site. Try it: all recipes, trending dishes, and the related recipes inside any recipe popup.

Alt text for this photo: “A plate of chicken biryani with saffron rice, fresh coriander and onion.” It describes the dish, not “IMG_1234” and not a list of stuffed keywords.

3. Audience & Industry · 4. Keyword Research

SEO starts with the audience, not with keywords. The industry is home cooking and food blogging for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, readers who cook at home, search in English, and often arrive from a phone. Keyword research then finds how that audience searches, not only what it searches for.

Who we are up against

  • Industry: desi home cooking, everyday dinners, festive dishes, street food and desserts.
  • Top competitors: large, long-running recipe sites and food blogs, recipe YouTube channels, and Pinterest food accounts. They do business mainly on Google Search, YouTube and Pinterest.
  • How they execute SEO: hundreds of tested recipes, strong photos and video, clear recipe pages, Pinterest pins for every dish, and years of links from other food sites.
  • Fiercest competition: the biggest recipe sites for the most-searched dishes, biryani, karahi, nihari. A new site does not beat them on day one by copying them; it wins first on more specific searches (see keyword research), better photos, and faster, clearer pages.
  • Where Zaika Worlds can win early: specific, practical searches, “easy”, “30 minutes”, ingredient and occasion searches, plus Pinterest, where a good vertical photo can travel further than a new site’s Google ranking.

How the audience searches and the variations that matter

  • How, not only what: the same dish is searched as “chicken biryani recipe”, “how to make chicken biryani”, “chicken biryani ingredients” and “easy chicken biryani”. A small change in wording can mean a very different reader.
  • Time-led: “30-minute chicken karahi”, “quick daal recipe”.
  • Ingredient-led: “biryani without yoghurt”, “nihari masala recipe”.
  • Occasion-led: “Eid dessert recipes”, “Ramadan iftar recipes”, “winter gajar ka halwa”.
  • Personas: a beginner who needs every step explained; a busy parent who needs the time first; a festive cook who needs quantities for 8–10 people. One page must serve all three, time and servings at the top, full detail below.
  • Market shifts: searches change with season and festivals. Keyword research is repeated, not done once, see Analytics & Reporting below.

2. Technical SEO · 8. Core Web Vitals · 9. Crawling · 10. Indexing · 11. Advanced Technical SEO

Technical SEO is the backend work that lets Google’s bots find, read and trust the site. If this is wrong, good content can stay invisible. The checks below are done before launch and re-checked after every major change.

LCP ≤ 2.5s

Largest Contentful Paint: loading

How fast the main content appears. What helps here: the hero photo is compressed and sized, and every image carries width and height so the page does not jump while loading.

INP ≤ 200ms

Interaction to Next Paint: interactivity

How fast the page responds when tapped or typed in. INP is the current metric, it replaced First Input Delay (FID) in March 2024. What helps here: search and filters that respond at once, with no heavy script blocking the tap.

CLS ≤ 0.1

Cumulative Layout Shift: visual stability

How much the page moves as it loads. What helps here: reserved space for images and no late-loading banners pushing the recipe text down.

Foundations, crawling and indexing: do

  • Speed: 2–3 seconds to load on a normal mobile connection. Slow sites lose readers before the recipe is even seen.
  • HTTPS (SSL): the whole site on secure HTTPS, with the lock in the address bar, and every old HTTP link redirected to HTTPS.
  • Crawlable architecture: home page → category → recipe, with internal links at every step. No recipe should be reachable only from search.
  • XML sitemap: a sitemap listing every recipe and category page, submitted in Google Search Console.
  • Robots.txt that allows crawling: User-agent: *
    Allow: /    , never Disallow: /, which blocks every search engine from the whole site.
  • Fix 404s and broken links: dead links are checked regularly; a moved page gets a redirect to its new address, not a dead end.
  • Canonical URLs: one chosen address per page. If a page can be reached with and without a trailing slash, one version is canonical and the other redirects to it, so Google does not index the same recipe twice or three times.
  • Schema markup (JSON-LD): structured data that tells Google “this is a recipe, these are its ingredients, this is its time.” This demo already carries Blog structured data in its code; the real site adds Recipe, Breadcrumb and FAQ schema to every recipe page, with names and facts spelled exactly as on the page.
  • JavaScript done safely: recipe text lives in the page itself, not only inside a script, so bots and readers without JavaScript still get the content.

Indexing: do not

  • Do not noindex recipe pages. noindex means “do not put this in search”, it belongs only on pages that should never rank, such as internal search-result pages.
  • Do not use nofollow on your own internal links by accident. It tells Google not to follow the link, which breaks the crawling path to your own recipes.
  • Do not leave a staging / test version public with the same content as the live site, that is duplicate content waiting to happen.
  • Do not submit a sitemap and forget it. New recipes go into the sitemap when they are published, and Search Console is checked for crawl errors.
  • Do not rely on “Fetch as Google” instead of a sitemap. It can help one page; a sitemap covers the whole site, every time.

7. Mobile SEO

  • Mobile-first, responsive design: one site, one URL, that reflows to any screen, Google indexes the mobile version first.
  • No m-dot site (m.example.com): separate mobile sites create duplicate content and canonical problems. This site uses one responsive layout instead.
  • Works on iPhone and Android: tested on both, with text readable without zooming, buttons big enough to tap, and the recipe popup usable on a small screen.
  • Same content on mobile and desktop: nothing important is hidden on mobile only.

6. Analytics and Reporting: accurate, or it is guesswork

If reporting is wrong, every adjustment after it is wrong too. Reports must separate real readers from bots, and must connect each result to the keyword and page that caused it.

Attribute everything

Every visit is tied to the keyword and the landing page that brought it. That shows which recipes actually drive traffic, and which only look popular.

Count humans, not bots

A report showing thousands of visits that are mostly bot traffic is not success. Bot and spam traffic is filtered out before any decision is made.

Refresh keywords regularly

Food search moves with seasons and festivals. Keyword research is repeated quarterly, and before Ramadan, Eid and winter, so the site follows what people are searching for next, instead of last year’s list.

What is reported each month: visits and clicks by landing page, top keywords by page, Core Web Vitals, crawl errors, and Pinterest saves and outbound clicks. One page of numbers, with one decision at the end, what to write, fix or update next.

12. Content · 13. Links

Content earns the links, and links lift the content. Neither works if the other is weak.

Content that earns its place

  • Manually written, authoritative: every recipe is written by a person who has cooked it, real quantities, real steps, real tips. “Just create great content” on its own is not a plan; content also needs the keyword, the headings, the image and the internal links done right.
  • Keyword targeting without stuffing: the main keyword in the title, H1 and first paragraph, variations in headings and steps, written so a person would never notice the optimisation.
  • No typos, no grammar errors, optimised images: compressed photos, correct sizes, descriptive file names and alt text.
  • Made to be shared and linked to: a clear tip, a useful variation, or a method detail another site would want to cite. If a piece flops despite being well written, it is reviewed and improved, not deleted in a panic, and not left to rot.

Links: quality only, varied by design

  • Quality over quantity: links from real, relevant food sites and publications, not bought links, link farms or spammy directories.
  • No spammy footprint: one bad link now and then is not fatal; saturating the link profile with bad links is. Every link opportunity is judged before it is taken.
  • A varied profile: recipe links, food-media mentions, YouTube descriptions, Pinterest pins and internal links, not one type, from one place, with the same anchor text every time.
  • Outbound links too: where a source is cited, such as the photo credits at the foot of this page, it links to the real original page.

15. Modern Search: GEO, Programmatic SEO, AXO, AEO, AISEO, LLMO and more

Search is no longer only ten blue links. People now ask AI chatbots, voice assistants and visual search, and the answer often arrives without a click. Each modern form below is covered, with what Zaika Worlds does about it.

GEO : Generative Engine Optimisation

Being cited inside AI-generated answers (AI Overviews, chatbots). What we do: clear facts, direct answers, original tips and Recipe schema, so an AI can quote the page accurately and link to it.

AEO : Answer Engine Optimisation

Winning the direct answer, featured snippets and “People also ask”. What we do: the answer in the first paragraph, short Q&A sections, and step lists Google can lift word-for-word.

AISEO : AI Search Optimisation

Optimising for AI search engines as a whole, not only Google. What we do: the same clean structure, facts and schema that serve Google also serve AI search, one source of truth, kept up to date.

LLMO : Large Language Model Optimisation

Being the source large language models learn from and cite. What we do: plain, factual, well-structured text with named quantities, times and steps, easy for a model to quote without inventing details.

AXO : AI Experience Optimisation

How the answer feels inside an AI chat or voice reply, and what the reader is offered next. What we do: short answers first, full detail one click away, and follow-up questions answered on the same page (variations, storage, serving).

Programmatic SEO

Many useful pages built from a template and real data, not hand-writing hundreds of near-identical pages. What we do: only where each page is genuinely different (a different dish, time or ingredient), each with its own intro, photo and steps. No thin, auto-generated duplicates.

Voice Search

“How long does biryani take?” spoken, not typed. What we do: conversational Q&A, times and quantities stated plainly, and FAQ schema.

Visual & Video Search

Google Lens, Pinterest search, and YouTube. What we do: descriptive alt text and file names, tall Pinterest pins for every recipe, and YouTube videos with chapters, descriptions and links back to the recipe page.

The rule across all of them is the same: one accurate, well-structured page, written for a person, that machines can also read. Chasing each new acronym with separate trick pages would weaken the site, not strengthen it.

14. Taking Action: the most important factor

A plan that is never implemented is worth nothing. The work below turns this playbook into a launch, in order. Items marked “in this demo” are already done on this page; the rest are done on the real domain, before and just after launch.

  1. Week 1: Foundations. Choose the site name, domain and hosting. Set up HTTPS, the responsive design, one H1 per page, alt text, internal linking and Recipe JSON-LD. In this demo: responsive layout, headings, alt text, internal links and Blog schema are already in place.
  2. Week 1: Crawl & index setup. Write robots.txt (Allow: /), build the XML sitemap, submit it in Google Search Console, pick one canonical URL form, and fix any 404s and redirects.
  3. Week 2: First 10 recipes. Publish the trending dishes first (see Trending), each with a title under 60 characters, a meta description, a vertical Pinterest pin, and a YouTube video where one exists.
  4. Week 3: Measure. Turn on analytics with bot filtering, check Core Web Vitals on a real phone, and fix the slowest page first. Record which keyword and landing page brings each visit.
  5. Week 4: Expand and refresh. Add the next 10 recipes, pin every recipe on Pinterest, start one programmatic set only where pages are genuinely different, and write the first AI-answer Q&A blocks for the top 5 recipes.
  6. Every quarter: repeat keyword research. Check what changed, update the top pages, retire or rewrite anything thin or outdated, and report one decision per month: what to write, fix or update next.

Honest note: this demo preview cannot do everything on the list by itself, it has no live domain, Search Console account, sitemap file or server redirects yet, and it does not save data. Those are set up on the real website, as described in the full plan. What the demo does show, working, is the design, the recipes, the headings, the alt text, the internal links and the speed-friendly layout that the real site will be built on. No traffic or ranking is promised, it depends on the work above, done consistently.
