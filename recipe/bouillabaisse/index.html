Bouillabaisse

75 min · 6 people · World Food · France

Published on 28 October 2026

Bouillabaisse comes from Marseille, where fishermen made soup from the fish that did not sell, and it is now one of France's most loved celebration dishes. The broth is the star, built from fish stock, tomatoes, fennel, orange peel, saffron and olive oil, and the fish and shellfish cook in it for only a few minutes, so they stay tender. Serve it in two parts if you can, broth strained into bowls and fish on a platter, with rouille, a saffron garlic mayonnaise, spread on toasted bread. It sounds grand, but the method is simple, make a good broth, add the fish in the right order, and do not overcook it.

Ingredients

  • Mixed firm white fish 800g (cut into chunks)
  • Prawns 300g
  • mussels 300g (cleaned)
  • Fish stock 1.5 litres
  • Tomatoes 400g (chopped)
  • tomato paste 2 tbsp
  • Fennel 1 bulb
  • onion 1
  • leek 1
  • Saffron 1 large pinch
  • orange peel 1 strip
  • Garlic 4 cloves
  • olive oil 4 tbsp
  • thyme
  • Crusty bread
  • rouille
  • parsley
  • lemon to serve

Method

  1. Heat olive oil in a large pot, fry the onion, leek, fennel and garlic for 8 minutes until soft and sweet.
  2. Add the tomato paste and chopped tomatoes, and cook for 5 minutes until the tomatoes start to break down.
  3. Pour in the fish stock, add saffron, orange peel, thyme and a pinch of salt, and simmer for 20 minutes.
  4. Strain the broth through a sieve, pressing the vegetables to get all the flavour, then return the broth to the pot.
  5. Bring the broth back to a gentle simmer, and add the firmest fish first, cooking for 3 minutes.
  6. Add the remaining fish and the prawns, and cook for 3 to 4 minutes more, until just cooked through.
  7. Add the mussels, cover, and cook for 3 minutes until they open. Discard any that stay closed.
  8. Ladle the broth and seafood into warm bowls, and serve with toasted bread spread with rouille, parsley and lemon.

Chef's tip

Do not overcook the fish. Add the firmest pieces first and the delicate pieces last, and take the pot off the heat as soon as the fish flakes.

Serving

Serves 6, in deep warm bowls, with the fish and shellfish arranged on top, and a plate of rouille topped toast in the middle of the table. Each person spoons rouille into the broth or spreads it on bread, and squeezes lemon over the fish. Serve with a green salad and a glass of dry white wine, and keep the bread coming, because the broth is too good to leave.

Storage and reheating

Bouillabaisse is best eaten fresh, the day it is made. The broth on its own keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days and freezes well for 1 month. Leftover fish does not reheat well and can turn rubbery, so store the broth separately and add fresh fish when you reheat. Reheat the broth until boiling, then cook the seafood fresh in it.

Ingredient notes

  • Saffron is expensive, but a pinch transforms the broth with colour and aroma. Soak it in a spoon of hot stock for 10 minutes before adding it.
  • Use a mix of fish, such as monkfish, sea bass and red mullet if you can find them. One fish alone makes a thinner soup.
  • Orange peel adds a bright, fragrant note that is classic in Marseille. Use a wide strip with no white pith, and remove it before serving.
  • Rouille is made by whisking egg yolk, garlic, saffron, lemon and olive oil into a mayonnaise. Shop bought aioli with saffron is a fair shortcut.

Variations

  • With clams: add cleaned clams with the mussels, and discard any that do not open.
  • Simpler weeknight version: use only prawns and one firm white fish, with the same broth, and serve with bread.
  • With potatoes: add diced potatoes to the broth and simmer until tender before adding the fish, for a heartier soup.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the seafood. Fish and prawns go from tender to tough in a minute, so watch the pot closely.
  • Skipping the straining step. The broth stays chunky and the flavour is less clear, so strain and press the vegetables.
  • Using oily fish. Salmon and mackerel overpower the delicate broth, so use firm white fish.
  • Adding all the fish at once. Delicate fish breaks up while firm fish is still cooking, so add in stages.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use shop bought fish stock?

Yes. Choose a good quality stock, and simmer it with the vegetables and saffron as written. It will still taste rich.

What is rouille?

A saffron and garlic mayonnaise from Provence, spread on toast and stirred into the soup. It adds richness and colour.

Can I make bouillabaisse without shellfish?

Yes, use only fish, and add a little extra fish stock. The soup is still excellent, though less varied.

Is saffron essential?

It gives the classic colour and aroma. Without it, the soup is still good, but it is not quite bouillabaisse.
