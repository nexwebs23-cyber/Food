Hummus

20 min · 6 people · World Food · Middle East

Published on 28 October 2026

Good hummus is all about texture, silky, light and whipped, never grainy or heavy. It comes from the Levant, where chickpeas, tahini, lemon and garlic are blended with ice water until the dip turns pale and fluffy, then finished with olive oil, paprika and whole chickpeas. The secret is to blend the tahini first, on its own, until it is completely smooth, and to use warm chickpeas, or chickpeas with their skins rubbed off, for the smoothest result. Serve it as part of a mezze spread, with warm pitta, olives and salad, and let everyone scoop and share.

Ingredients

  • Chickpeas 400g (cooked)
  • drained
  • Tahini 120g (well stirred)
  • Lemon juice 3 tbsp
  • garlic 1 small clove
  • Ice water 4 to 6 tbsp
  • Olive oil 2 tbsp (plus more to serve)
  • Salt 1 tsp
  • cumin 1/2 tsp (optional)
  • Paprika
  • parsley to serve
  • Warm pitta bread to serve

Method

  1. If using tinned chickpeas, warm them in their liquid for 5 minutes, drain, and keep a little liquid. Rub off loose skins if you have time.
  2. Blend the tahini, lemon juice, garlic and salt for 2 minutes until very smooth, pale and slightly fluffy.
  3. Add the warm chickpeas and blend for 3 to 4 minutes, scraping down the sides, until completely smooth.
  4. Add ice water a tablespoon at a time, blending after each, until the hummus is light, whipped and soft enough to scoop.
  5. Taste and adjust the lemon, salt and garlic. It should be bright and savoury, with tahini flavour, not sharp.
  6. Spoon into a shallow bowl, swirl the top with the back of a spoon, and drizzle with olive oil.
  7. Top with a few whole chickpeas, a pinch of paprika and chopped parsley.
  8. Serve at room temperature with warm pitta, vegetable sticks and olives.

Chef's tip

Blend the tahini first, before the chickpeas. Tahini blended alone turns pale and creamy, and that is what makes the hummus silky.

Serving

Serves 6, as part of a mezze spread, with warm pitta, olives, pickles, salad and other dips. Serve the hummus in a wide shallow bowl, with the olive oil pooled in the swirl, and let everyone tear pitta and scoop. It is also excellent as a sandwich spread, a base for roasted vegetables, or a side with grilled meat and falafel.

Storage and reheating

Hummus keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days in an airtight container, with a thin layer of olive oil on top. Bring it back to room temperature before serving, and stir well. It freezes well for up to 3 months, thawed overnight in the fridge, then whisked smooth again. Do not leave it at room temperature for more than 2 hours.

Ingredient notes

  • Tahini quality matters most. Use a runny, well stirred tahini made from hulled sesame seeds. Thick, dry tahini makes heavy hummus.
  • Ice water whips air into the hummus and loosens it without adding oil. Add it slowly, or the hummus turns runny.
  • Garlic should be small and fresh. One large raw clove can overpower the whole bowl, so start small.
  • Warm chickpeas blend smoother than cold ones, because their starch is soft. If using cold chickpeas, warm them first.

Variations

  • Roasted red pepper hummus: blend in 1 roasted red pepper, and reduce the lemon a little.
  • Beetroot hummus: blend in 1 small cooked beetroot for colour and sweetness, a beautiful pink version.
  • Spicy hummus: add 1 tsp of harissa or chilli paste with the tahini, and top with chilli oil.

Common mistakes

  • Blending everything at once. The tahini never gets smooth, so blend it first on its own.
  • Using cold, dry chickpeas. They blend grainy, so warm them and add ice water slowly.
  • Adding too much garlic. Raw garlic grows stronger as hummus sits, so use a little and taste.
  • Serving straight from the fridge. Cold hummus is stiff and dull, so let it warm up first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use dried chickpeas?

Yes. Soak overnight, cook until very soft with a pinch of bicarbonate of soda, and use warm. They give the smoothest hummus.

Why is my hummus grainy?

The tahini was not blended first, the chickpeas were cold, or the skins were left on. Blend longer and add ice water.

Can I make hummus without tahini?

You can, with extra olive oil and a little sesame oil, but it will not taste like classic hummus. Tahini is the heart of the dish.

Is hummus healthy?

It is made from chickpeas, tahini, lemon and olive oil, with no numbers added here. The oil and salt are the parts to watch, so serve it with vegetables and pitta.
