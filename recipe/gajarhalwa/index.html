Gajar Ka Halwa

55 min · 5 people · Desserts · India

Gajar ka halwa is winter in a bowl, red carrots cooked slowly in milk until the milk disappears, then fried in ghee with sugar and finished with khoya, cardamom and nuts. It is not a quick dessert, and that is the point. The long, gentle cooking turns grated carrot into something soft, rich and deeply flavoured, with no shortcuts that taste the same. Use red carrots when you can find them, grate them finely, and keep the heat low. Serve it warm, with a spoon of khoya on top, and save some for the next day, because it tastes even better then.

Ingredients

  • Red carrots 1 kg (grated)
  • Milk 1 litre
  • sugar 1 cup
  • Ghee 4 tbsp
  • khoya 150g
  • Cardamom 4
  • almonds / pistachios

Method

  1. Wash, peel and grate the carrots finely. Fine grating matters, because thick shreds take longer and stay coarse.
  2. Put the carrots and milk in a heavy kadhai. Bring to a boil, then reduce to low heat.
  3. Cook uncovered on low heat for 35 to 45 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until the milk has almost dried up.
  4. Add the ghee and fry the carrots, stirring, for 8 to 10 minutes, until the mixture turns glossy and leaves the sides.
  5. Add the sugar and cook for 8 to 10 minutes more, stirring, until the sugar melts and the halwa thickens again.
  6. Crush the cardamom and add it with the khoya. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring, until the khoya melts into the halwa.
  7. Taste and adjust. If it is too sweet, a squeeze of lemon is not traditional, so instead serve smaller portions with unsweetened chai.
  8. Serve warm, topped with chopped almonds and pistachios and a little extra khoya.

Chef's tip

Grate the carrots finely. Thick carrots take longer to cook and the halwa stays coarse, no matter how long you stir it, so take the time to grate well at the start.

Serving

Serves 5, in small bowls, warm, with chopped nuts and a spoon of khoya on top. Gajar ka halwa is a winter dessert, served after dinner or with afternoon chai. A scoop of vanilla ice cream on warm halwa is a popular modern serving, and the contrast of hot and cold works beautifully. Keep portions small, because it is rich.

Storage and reheating

Cool and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Reheat in a pan on low heat with a splash of milk, stirring, until warm through. It also reheats well in the microwave in short bursts. Freeze for up to 1 month in portions, thaw in the fridge, and reheat with a little milk. Add fresh nuts at serving, because stored nuts soften.

Ingredient notes

  • Red carrots are sweeter and give the deep colour. Orange carrots work, but the halwa is paler and needs a little more sugar.
  • Full fat milk gives a richer result. Low fat milk takes longer to reduce and tastes thinner.
  • Khoya at the end adds richness and body. It is worth buying good khoya, or crumbling paneer as a substitute in a pinch.
  • Cardamom should be freshly crushed. Pre ground cardamom loses its aroma quickly.

Variations

  • Milk powder halwa: replace half the milk with 1 cup of milk powder mixed into the carrots, for a faster, richer version.
  • Nutty halwa: add chopped cashews and raisins with the khoya, and toast the nuts in ghee first.
  • Less sweet halwa: reduce the sugar to 3/4 cup. The halwa is still sweet from the carrots, and suits serving with ice cream.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on high heat. The milk catches and burns, and the halwa tastes scorched.
  • Adding sugar too early. It draws out water and the carrots boil instead of reducing, so add it after the milk dries.
  • Grating thickly. The halwa stays stringy and coarse, however long it cooks.
  • Skipping the ghee frying stage. Without it, the halwa tastes boiled, not rich and glossy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use orange carrots?

Yes. The halwa is paler and a little less sweet, so taste and add 1 to 2 tbsp more sugar if needed. Red carrots are traditional for winter halwa.

How do I know the milk has reduced enough?

The mixture looks dry, the carrots hold together, and you see ghee starting to separate at the edges when you stir. That is the time to add the ghee and fry.

Can I make it without khoya?

Yes. It is still good, just less rich. Add 2 extra tbsp of ghee and 2 tbsp of milk powder at the end, or crumble in soft paneer.

Is gajar ka halwa served hot or cold?

Warm is best, straight from the kadhai or gently reheated. Cold halwa from the fridge is firm and less fragrant, so warm it before serving.
