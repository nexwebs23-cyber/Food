Dim Sum

Jump to Recipe
Time60 min
Servings4 people
DifficultyMedium
CategoryWorld Food · China

Published on 28 October 2026

Dim sum means food that touches the heart, and it is meant to be shared, small bamboo steamers arriving at the table one after another, each with a different bite inside. This home version makes two classics, prawn har gow with translucent wrappers, and pork and prawn siu mai with open tops, both steamed until just cooked and served hot. The wrappers need a light touch and a little patience, but the filling is simple, and the steaming is quick. Make them with help, one person filling, one person folding, and keep the finished dumplings covered so they do not dry out. Serve with tea, as in Cantonese yum cha, and let the meal stretch out slowly.

Ingredients and method

Ingredients

  • Prawns 300g (finely chopped)
  • Pork mince 200g
  • Wheat starch 120g
  • tapioca starch 60g (for har gow)
  • Wonton wrappers 20 (for siu mai)
  • Ginger 1 tbsp (grated)
  • spring onions 2
  • Soy sauce 2 tbsp
  • sesame oil 1 tsp
  • white pepper
  • Sugar 1 tsp
  • Shaoxing wine 1 tbsp
  • Chilli oil
  • soy sauce
  • black vinegar to serve

Method

  1. Mix the prawns, pork, ginger, spring onions, soy, sesame oil, sugar, wine and white pepper into a filling, and chill for 20 minutes.
  2. For har gow wrappers, mix wheat starch and tapioca starch with 180ml of just boiled water, knead into a smooth dough, and rest covered for 15 minutes.
  3. Roll the har gow dough into a log, cut into small pieces, and press each one into a thin round with the side of a cleaver or a rolling pin.
  4. Place a spoon of filling in each wrapper, fold into a crescent, and pleat the edge firmly, sealing the top.
  5. For siu mai, place a spoon of filling in the centre of each wonton wrapper, gather the edges up around the filling, and press gently so the top stays open.
  6. Line bamboo steamers with baking paper with holes, or cabbage leaves, and arrange the dumplings with space between them.
  7. Steam har gow for 6 to 7 minutes and siu mai for 8 minutes, until the wrappers are translucent or set and the filling is cooked.
  8. Serve at once in the steamers, with chilli oil, soy sauce and black vinegar for dipping, and hot tea on the side.

Chef's tip

Do not overfill the dumplings. A level teaspoon of filling seals neatly, while too much filling bursts the wrapper in the steamer.

Good to know

Serving

Serves 4, as part of a shared meal, with 5 to 6 dumplings per person, plus tea, and a simple cucumber salad or stir fried greens on the side. Serve the steamers straight to the table, lid on, so the dumplings stay hot and steamy until the moment they are eaten. Dim sum is slow food in small bites, so refill the tea, bring the steamers out in batches, and do not rush.

Storage and reheating

Uncooked dumplings freeze well for up to 1 month on a tray, then bagged. Steam from frozen for 3 to 4 minutes longer. Cooked dumplings keep in the fridge for 1 day, and reheat by steaming for 3 minutes, not in the microwave, which toughens the wrappers. Leftover filling keeps for 1 day in the fridge, covered.

Ingredient notes

  • Wheat starch makes har gow wrappers translucent and delicate. Plain flour will not give the same result, so buy wheat starch from an Asian shop.
  • Keep the dough covered at all times. It dries in minutes and cracks when you try to fold it.
  • Chill the filling before shaping. Cold filling is easier to portion and holds its shape in the wrapper.
  • Steam in batches if needed. Crowded steamers stick together, and the dumplings tear when you lift them.

Variations

  • Vegetable dim sum: replace the meat with finely chopped mushrooms, cabbage, carrot and tofu, seasoned the same way.
  • Chicken dim sum: use chicken mince with prawns, and add water chestnuts for crunch.
  • With shop bought wrappers: use round dumpling wrappers for both types, pleated closed, for an easier version that still tastes excellent.

Common mistakes

  • Using wrappers that are too thick. They stay doughy after steaming, so roll or press them thin.
  • Steaming on a dry steamer. The dumplings stick and tear, so line the steamer and oil it lightly.
  • Opening the lid constantly. The steam escapes and the dumplings cook unevenly, so keep the lid on until the time is up.
  • Making the dumplings too far ahead. The wrappers dry out and crack, so shape them just before steaming, or keep them covered with a damp cloth.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make dim sum without a bamboo steamer?

Yes. Use a metal steamer, a colander over a pot, or a plate on a rack in a covered wok. Line it well so nothing sticks.

Why are my har gow wrappers cracking?

The dough dried out, or it cooled too much. Keep it covered, work with small pieces, and knead it briefly if it stiffens.

Can I use frozen prawns?

Yes. Thaw them fully, pat them dry, and chop them just before mixing. Wet prawns make a loose filling.

What sauce goes with dim sum?

Chilli oil, light soy sauce and black vinegar, mixed to taste. A little grated ginger in the vinegar is traditional.
