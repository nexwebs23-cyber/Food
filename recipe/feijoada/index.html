Feijoada

180 min · 8 people · World Food · Brazil

Feijoada is Brazil's national dish, a rich black bean stew with a mix of pork, sausage and smoked meats, cooked slowly until the beans are creamy and the meats are tender. It is traditionally served on Saturdays, as a long family lunch, with white rice, sauteed collard greens, farofa, toasted cassava flour, and orange slices to cut the richness. It takes time, and it feeds a crowd, so cook a big pot, keep the accompaniments simple, and let the meal stretch out. Soak the beans overnight, brown the meats well, and give the pot a long, gentle simmer, and the result is deeply satisfying.

Ingredients

  • Dried black beans 500g, soaked overnight
  • Pork shoulder 500g, cubed
  • Smoked sausage 300g, sliced
  • Bacon or salt pork 200g, diced
  • Onion 1, garlic 4 cloves
  • Bay leaves 3, cumin 1 tsp, oregano 1 tsp
  • Orange 1, for serving, rice and collard greens
  • Farofa or toasted breadcrumbs to serve, optional

Method

  1. Soak the beans overnight in cold water, drain, and rinse.
  2. Brown the pork, sausage and bacon in batches in a large pot, then set aside, keeping the fat in the pot.
  3. Fry the onion and garlic in the same pot until soft, then add the cumin and oregano.
  4. Return the meats to the pot, add the beans, bay leaves and enough water to cover by 3cm.
  5. Bring to a boil, skim, then reduce to a very gentle simmer, partly covered, for 2 to 2 and a half hours.
  6. Stir occasionally, topping up with hot water if needed, until the beans are creamy and the meats are tender.
  7. Taste and season with salt and pepper. The meats add salt, so taste before adding more.
  8. Serve in deep bowls with white rice, sauteed collard greens, farofa and orange slices on the side.

Tip

Soak the beans overnight and simmer gently. Rushed beans stay hard, and a hard boil breaks them into mush instead of creamy beans.

Serving

Serves 8, as a long family lunch, with white rice, collard greens sauteed with garlic, farofa and orange slices in the middle of the table. Each person serves themselves beans and meat over rice, adds greens and farofa, and eats orange between bites to refresh the palate. Serve with a cold drink and keep the pot on the stove, because second helpings are expected.

Storage

Feijoada keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days, and the flavour improves overnight. Reheat gently until bubbling, adding a splash of water if it has thickened. It freezes very well for up to 3 months, in portions, thawed overnight in the fridge. Rice, greens and farofa should be made fresh, or stored separately for 2 days.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use tinned black beans?

Yes, in a pinch. Add them for the last 30 minutes with the browned meats, and reduce the water, but the stew will be less rich.

Is feijoada very fatty?

It is a rich stew, made with pork and sausage. Use leaner cuts and drain excess fat after browning if you prefer a lighter version.

What is farofa?

Toasted cassava flour, often with onion and bacon, sprinkled over the beans and rice. Toasted breadcrumbs are a fair substitute.

Can I make feijoada ahead?

Yes, and it is better the next day. Cook it fully, cool, refrigerate, and reheat gently.
