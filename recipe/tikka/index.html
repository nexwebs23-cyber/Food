Chicken Tikka

45 min · 4 people · BBQ · Pakistan

Published on 28 October 2026

Chicken tikka is the dish that teaches patience in a good way. The marinade does the work, yoghurt and spices working into the meat for at least an hour, and the grill only needs high heat and a few minutes per side. Done right, the outside is lightly charred and smoky, the inside stays juicy, and lemon and chaat masala at the end wake everything up. It works as a starter, as a main dish with naan, and sliced into wraps and salads the next day. The recipe below uses an oven grill or a barbecue, and a hot pan works too if that is what you have.

Ingredients

  • Chicken (leg / boneless) 750g
  • Yoghurt 1/2 cup
  • red chilli 1 tbsp
  • Turmeric
  • garam masala
  • cumin powder
  • Ginger-garlic paste 1 tbsp
  • lemon 1
  • Oil / butter for brushing

Method

  1. Cut the chicken into even pieces and score each piece with 2 shallow cuts, so the marinade reaches inside.
  2. Mix the yoghurt, red chilli, turmeric, garam masala, cumin powder, ginger-garlic paste, lemon juice and salt into a smooth paste.
  3. Coat the chicken well, cover, and refrigerate for at least 1 hour. Overnight is fine and gives a deeper flavour.
  4. Take the chicken out 20 minutes before cooking, so it is not fridge cold when it hits the heat.
  5. Heat the grill or barbecue to high. Oil the rack lightly and lay the chicken in a single layer, leaving space between pieces.
  6. Grill for 6 to 8 minutes on the first side, brushing with butter or oil once, until the edges char lightly.
  7. Turn and grill for 6 to 8 minutes more, until cooked through and the juices run clear.
  8. Rest for 5 minutes, sprinkle lemon juice and chaat masala, and serve hot with onion, chutney and naan.

Chef's tip

The longer the marination, the softer the tikka. If time is short, give it at least 30 minutes, and never skip the scoring, because that is how the marinade gets into thick pieces.

Serving

Serves 4 with naan or roti, sliced onion, lemon wedges, green chutney and a salad. Serve on a warm platter, straight from the grill, with the lemon squeezed over at the table. Tikka also sits well on rice, in a wrap, or on top of a salad. Keep extra chutney on the side, because people always ask for more.

Storage and reheating

Cool and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Reheat in a hot oven at 180C for 8 minutes, or in a pan on medium heat, until steaming hot. Do not microwave for long, because it dries the chicken and softens the char. Marinated, uncooked chicken keeps in the fridge for up to 1 day. Freeze cooked tikka for up to 1 month and thaw in the fridge.

Ingredient notes

  • Thigh meat stays juicier than breast on high heat. Breast works, but check it a minute earlier.
  • Hang the yoghurt or use thick yoghurt. Watery yoghurt slides off the chicken and burns on the grill.
  • Fresh lemon in the marinade helps tenderise, but too much lemon for too long can make the surface mushy, so keep overnight marinades lemon light.
  • Chaat masala goes on at the end, not in the marinade, so its tang stays bright.

Variations

  • Malai tikka: replace red chilli with white pepper, add 2 tbsp of cream and 1 tbsp of cheese to the marinade, for a mild, creamy version.
  • Hariyali tikka: blend coriander, mint, green chillies and yoghurt into a green marinade, and grill the same way.
  • Tikka wraps: slice leftover tikka, roll in paratha or roti with onion, chutney and salad, for lunch the next day.

Common mistakes

  • Grilling fridge cold chicken. The outside chars before the inside cooks, so bring it to room temperature first.
  • Crowding the grill. Pieces steam instead of charring, so leave space and cook in batches if needed.
  • Skipping the rest. Cutting at once lets the juices run out and the tikka dries.
  • Using a thin marinade. It drips off and burns, leaving the chicken bland.

Frequently asked questions

Can I cook tikka in a pan?

Yes. Use a heavy pan on high heat with a little oil, 6 to 7 minutes per side. It will not have the same smoky char, but the flavour is close.

How do I get the smoky flavour at home?

After grilling, place a small steel bowl with a hot charcoal piece in the pot, drizzle a few drops of oil on it, cover for 2 minutes, and remove the bowl. Keep the kitchen ventilated.

Can I use breast chicken?

Yes, but do not overcook it. Check after 5 to 6 minutes per side, and rest it well. Thigh is more forgiving.

Is tikka spicy?

It is warm and spiced, not fiercely hot. Reduce the red chilli to 1 tsp for a mild version, and let the chutney add heat at the table.
