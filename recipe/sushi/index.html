Sushi

50 min · 4 people · World Food · Japan

Published on 28 October 2026

Sushi is about rice first and fish second. In Japan, the rice is seasoned with vinegar, sugar and salt, fanned cool, and handled gently so every grain stays separate and glossy. This home version makes salmon and cucumber rolls, the kind that are easiest to learn, with nori on the outside, rice in the middle, and a neat line of filling. Use sushi grade salmon, keep your hands wet, and roll firmly but gently. The first roll may look uneven, and that is normal. By the third roll, the shape, the cut and the confidence all come together, and the result tastes fresh, clean and far better than shop bought sushi that has sat in a fridge.

Ingredients

  • Sushi rice 2 cups (uncooked)
  • Rice vinegar 4 tbsp
  • sugar 2 tbsp
  • salt 1 tsp
  • Nori sheets 4
  • Sushi grade salmon 300g (cut into strips)
  • Cucumber 1 (cut into thin sticks)
  • Mayonnaise 3 tbsp
  • sriracha 1 tsp (for spicy mayo)
  • Soy sauce
  • pickled ginger
  • wasabi to serve
  • Sesame seeds 1 tbsp (optional)

Nutrition (approx. per serving)

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water runs clear, cook it, then rest it covered for 10 minutes.
  2. Warm the vinegar, sugar and salt until dissolved, fold it into the hot rice with a slicing motion, and fan the rice until it is just warm and glossy.
  3. Lay a nori sheet shiny side down on a bamboo mat, wet your hands, and spread a thin even layer of rice, leaving 2cm bare at the far edge.
  4. Spread a thin line of spicy mayo across the centre, add salmon and cucumber sticks in a neat row, and sprinkle with sesame seeds.
  5. Lift the near edge of the mat and roll firmly away from you, tucking the filling in, and press gently to seal the bare edge with a little water.
  6. Rest the roll for 2 minutes, then cut it with a wet sharp knife into 8 pieces, wiping the blade between cuts.
  7. Repeat with the remaining rice and filling, keeping your hands wet throughout.
  8. Arrange the pieces cut side up, and serve with soy sauce, pickled ginger and a small amount of wasabi.

Chef's tip

Keep a bowl of water nearby and wet your hands and knife often. Wet hands stop the rice sticking, and a wet blade cuts cleanly without squashing the roll.

Serving

Serves 4 as a light meal, with 2 rolls per person, or as part of a larger Japanese meal with miso soup and salad. Serve at room temperature, soon after cutting, with soy sauce for dipping, pickled ginger to cleanse the palate between pieces, and wasabi used sparingly. Sushi is finger food, so set out chopsticks and small dishes, and let everyone serve themselves.

Storage and reheating

Sushi is best eaten the day it is made, within a few hours of rolling. Refrigeration dries the rice and dulls the fish, so do not make it a day ahead. If you must keep it, cover tightly and refrigerate for up to 6 hours, then bring it back to room temperature for 20 minutes before serving. Do not freeze sushi. Cooked sushi rice on its own keeps for 1 day in the fridge.

Ingredient notes

  • Sushi rice is short grain rice. Long grain rice does not stick in the same way, so do not substitute it if you want neat rolls.
  • Sushi grade means fish sold for raw eating, kept cold from catch to sale. Ask your fish seller, and do not use ordinary fish that is meant for cooking.
  • Rice vinegar is mild and sweet. Do not replace it with strong malt vinegar, which would overpower the rice.
  • Nori should be crisp and dark. Store it sealed and dry, because damp nori tears and will not roll neatly.

Variations

  • Tuna rolls: replace the salmon with sushi grade tuna, and add avocado instead of cucumber.
  • Cooked prawn rolls: use cooked prawns, cucumber and a little mayo, a good choice if you prefer no raw fish.
  • Vegetable rolls: use cucumber, avocado, carrot sticks and pickled radish, with sesame seeds, for a fully vegetarian roll.

Common mistakes

  • Using hot rice. It steams the nori soft and makes the roll fall apart, so cool the rice until just warm.
  • Overfilling the roll. Too much filling stops the roll closing, so keep the filling to one neat row.
  • Cutting with a dry knife. The rice sticks and drags, so wet the blade before every cut.
  • Pressing too hard while rolling. The rice squashes and the filling squeezes out, so roll firmly but gently.

Frequently asked questions

Is raw salmon safe to eat at home?

Only if it is sushi grade salmon from a trusted seller, kept cold, and eaten the same day. When in doubt, use cooked prawns or vegetables instead.

Can I make sushi without a bamboo mat?

Yes. Use a tea towel under cling film, or a sheet of baking paper. The mat makes it easier, but it is not essential.

Why is my rice sticky and mushy?

It was not rinsed enough, or it was stirred hard while seasoning. Rinse well and fold the seasoning in gently.

Can I prepare the rice ahead?

Yes, by a few hours. Keep it covered at room temperature, not in the fridge, and roll just before serving.
