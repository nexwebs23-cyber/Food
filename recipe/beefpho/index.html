Beef Pho

180 min · 6 people · World Food · Vietnam

Pho is Vietnam's most famous soup, and its heart is a clear broth that tastes of charred onion and ginger, star anise, cinnamon, cloves and fish sauce, simmered gently with beef bones until it is rich but never cloudy. The noodles cook separately, the beef is sliced paper thin and cooks in the hot broth as it is poured over, and the bowl is finished at the table with bean sprouts, Thai basil, coriander, lime and chilli. It takes time, but the work is mostly waiting and skimming, and the result is a bowl that is light, fragrant and deeply comforting, nothing like a heavy stew.

Ingredients

  • Beef bones 1.5kg
  • marrow or knuckle bones
  • Beef brisket or shin 500g
  • Rice noodles 500g (flat)
  • Onion 2
  • ginger 1 large piece
  • Star anise 3
  • cinnamon stick 1
  • cloves 4
  • Coriander seeds 1 tsp
  • fennel seeds 1 tsp
  • Fish sauce 4 tbsp
  • rock sugar 1 tbsp
  • Bean sprouts
  • Thai basil
  • coriander
  • lime
  • chilli to serve

Method

  1. Blanch the bones in boiling water for 5 minutes, drain, rinse, and return them to a clean pot with fresh cold water.
  2. Char the onion halves and ginger over an open flame or under a hot grill until blackened in patches, then add to the pot.
  3. Toast the star anise, cinnamon, cloves, coriander and fennel in a dry pan for 2 minutes until fragrant, tie in muslin or add loose and strain later.
  4. Simmer the broth very gently for 2 and a half hours, skimming often, and add the brisket for the last 60 minutes until tender.
  5. Lift out the brisket, cool slightly, slice thinly, and strain the broth through a fine sieve until clear.
  6. Season the broth with fish sauce and rock sugar, tasting as you go, until it is savoury, fragrant and just a little sweet.
  7. Cook the rice noodles separately, drain, and divide between bowls with slices of brisket and raw thin sliced beef.
  8. Ladle the boiling broth over each bowl, so the raw beef cooks in the heat, and serve with herbs, sprouts, lime and chilli.

Chef's tip

Keep the simmer gentle and skim often. A rolling boil makes the broth cloudy and greasy, while a quiet simmer keeps it clear and clean tasting.

Serving

Serves 6, in deep bowls, with the herbs, sprouts, lime and chilli in separate bowls in the middle of the table. Each person adds their own herbs and a squeeze of lime, and eats with chopsticks and a spoon, noodles first, then the broth. Pho is served steaming hot and eaten at once, while the herbs are still fresh and the beef is just cooked.

Storage and reheating

Broth keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days and freezes well for up to 3 months. Store the broth, noodles and toppings separately, and assemble fresh. Reheat the broth until boiling, cook fresh noodles, and slice the beef just before serving. Leftover cooked brisket keeps for 2 days in the fridge. Do not store assembled bowls.

Ingredient notes

  • Charring the onion and ginger adds a smoky sweetness that is essential to pho. Do not skip it, and do not peel the onion fully, the skin adds colour.
  • Rock sugar balances the fish sauce without making the broth taste sweet. Ordinary sugar works, but use a little less.
  • Slice the raw beef while it is partly frozen. It slices thinly and evenly, and cooks in seconds in the hot broth.
  • Rice noodles should be soaked or cooked until just tender, then drained well, so they do not soak up all the broth in the bowl.

Variations

  • Chicken pho: use a whole chicken and chicken bones, simmer for 60 to 75 minutes, and shred the meat for the bowls.
  • Vegetarian pho: use mushroom and vegetable stock with the same spices, charred onion and ginger, and tofu and mushrooms on top.
  • Spicier pho: add sliced fresh chilli and chilli sauce at the table, rather than in the broth, so each person controls the heat.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling the broth hard. It turns cloudy and the fat emulsifies into the soup, so keep it at a bare simmer.
  • Not skimming. Foam and fat left in the pot dull the flavour and the clarity.
  • Overcooking the noodles. They soak up the broth and turn mushy, so cook them separately and drain well.
  • Forgetting to taste the seasoning. Fish sauce and sugar must be balanced at the end, so season in small amounts and taste often.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make pho in a pressure cooker?

Yes. Cook the bones at pressure for 60 to 75 minutes, then strain and season. The flavour is very close, though the broth is a little less clear.

What beef is best for the raw slices?

Sirloin or eye of round, sliced very thin while partly frozen. It cooks in the hot broth in seconds.

Is pho gluten free?

Rice noodles and most fish sauce are naturally gluten free, but check your fish sauce and hoisin labels for additives.

Can I make the broth ahead?

Yes, and it improves overnight. Refrigerate, remove the solidified fat, and reheat until boiling.
