Peking Duck

180 min · 6 people · World Food · China

Peking duck is one of China's great celebration dishes, famous for skin so crisp it shatters, and meat that stays juicy underneath. The secret is drying the skin and glazing it before roasting, so the fat renders out and the skin turns a deep lacquered red gold. This home version uses an overnight rest in the fridge to dry the skin, a simple maltose and vinegar glaze, and a hot oven, followed by a rest before carving. Serve it the traditional way, in thin mandarin pancakes with hoisin sauce, spring onion and cucumber, and let everyone assemble their own. It is a project, not a weeknight dinner, and it is worth every minute.

Ingredients

  • Whole duck 2 to 2.5kg
  • Maltose or honey 3 tbsp, rice vinegar 2 tbsp
  • Shaoxing wine 2 tbsp, five spice 1 tsp
  • Salt 1 tbsp, boiling water for blanching
  • Mandarin pancakes 12
  • Hoisin sauce 120ml
  • Spring onions 4, cucumber 1
  • Ginger and garlic for the cavity, optional

Method

  1. Clean the duck, pat it very dry, and prick the skin lightly all over with a fork, taking care not to pierce the meat.
  2. Pour boiling water over the duck in the sink, which tightens the skin, then pat dry again.
  3. Warm the maltose, vinegar, Shaoxing wine, five spice and salt until the maltose dissolves, and brush it all over the duck.
  4. Rest the duck uncovered in the fridge overnight, on a rack over a tray, so the skin dries completely.
  5. Roast breast side up at 200C for 30 minutes, then reduce to 180C and roast for 60 to 75 minutes more, until the skin is deep golden and crisp.
  6. Baste once halfway with the remaining glaze, and cover the wing tips with foil if they brown too fast.
  7. Rest the duck for 20 minutes before carving, so the juices settle and the skin stays crisp.
  8. Carve the skin and meat into thin slices, warm the pancakes, and serve with hoisin, spring onion batons and cucumber sticks.

Tip

Dry the skin overnight. Wet skin steams in the oven and never crisps, while dry skin renders and lacquers beautifully.

Serving

Serves 6, as the centre of a shared meal, with the carved duck on a platter, warm pancakes, hoisin, spring onion and cucumber in the middle of the table. Each person spreads hoisin on a pancake, adds a few slices of duck and skin, a spring onion baton and cucumber, and rolls it up. Serve with a light soup or stir fried greens on the side, and keep the pancakes coming while the duck lasts.

Storage

Leftover duck keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days, stored in an airtight container. Reheat the meat gently in a covered pan with a splash of water, and crisp the skin separately under a hot grill or in a hot oven for a few minutes. Pancakes keep in the fridge for 2 days and reheat by steaming. Do not freeze the carved skin, it loses its crispness.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make Peking duck in a smaller oven?

Yes, if the duck fits with air around it. A smaller duck of about 2kg is easier, and the method is the same.

What if I cannot find maltose?

Use honey thinned with a little hot water. The glaze will be a little darker, so watch it near the end of roasting.

Can I use duck legs only?

Yes. Dry and glaze them the same way, and roast for about 50 to 60 minutes until crisp. They are easier to handle than a whole duck.

Why is my duck skin not crisp?

The skin was not dry enough, or the oven was too cool. Dry it overnight, prick it well, and roast hot.
