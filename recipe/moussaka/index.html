Moussaka

90 min · 6 people · World Food · Greece

Published on 28 October 2026

Moussaka is Greece's great layered bake, soft aubergine, a rich tomato and lamb or beef mince sauce scented with cinnamon, and a thick bechamel top that turns golden in the oven. It takes a little time, because each layer is cooked separately before the dish is assembled, but that is what gives it its structure and flavour, and it is best made ahead and rested, so the layers set and slice cleanly. Serve it warm, in squares, with a simple salad, and do not rush the resting time. It is a Sunday dish, generous and comforting, and it feeds a table happily.

Ingredients

  • Aubergines 2 large (sliced)
  • Potatoes 3 (sliced, optional)
  • Lamb or beef mince 600g
  • Onion 1
  • garlic 3 cloves
  • Tomatoes 400g (chopped)
  • tomato paste 2 tbsp
  • Cinnamon 1 tsp
  • oregano 1 tsp
  • nutmeg
  • Milk 500ml
  • butter 50g
  • flour 50g
  • eggs 2
  • Cheese 80g (grated)
  • kefalotyri or parmesan

Method

  1. Salt the aubergine slices and rest for 30 minutes, then pat dry and fry or roast until golden and tender. Set aside.
  2. Boil or roast the potato slices until just tender, if using, and set aside.
  3. Fry the onion and garlic until soft, add the mince, and brown well, breaking it up.
  4. Add the tomato paste, chopped tomatoes, cinnamon, oregano and seasoning, and simmer for 20 minutes until thick.
  5. Make the bechamel by melting butter, stirring in flour, and whisking in warm milk until thick and smooth. Cool slightly, then whisk in the eggs and half the cheese.
  6. Layer potatoes, then aubergine, then mince sauce in a buttered baking dish, repeating once, and finish with the bechamel.
  7. Sprinkle with the remaining cheese and a little nutmeg, and bake at 180C for 40 to 45 minutes until golden and bubbling.
  8. Rest for 20 minutes before slicing, so the layers set, and serve warm with salad.

Chef's tip

Rest the moussaka before cutting. Hot from the oven it falls apart, rested it slices into clean, beautiful layers.

Serving

Serves 6, in generous squares, with a simple Greek salad of tomato, cucumber, onion and olives, and crusty bread on the side. Serve it warm, not piping hot, so the flavours have settled and the layers hold. Moussaka is a sharing dish, so bring the baking dish to the table, and serve with extra salad and bread for anyone who wants more.

Storage and reheating

Moussaka keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days, covered, and reheats well in the oven at 160C until hot through. It freezes well for up to 3 months, in portions, thawed overnight in the fridge. Reheat covered with foil so the top does not overbrown. Do not microwave a whole dish, the bechamel can split.

Ingredient notes

  • Salting the aubergine draws out bitter water and stops it soaking up too much oil. Pat it dry before frying.
  • Cinnamon is the signature spice in Greek mince sauce. Use it lightly, so it warms the sauce without tasting sweet.
  • Bechamel must be thick enough to sit on top. If it is thin, it sinks into the mince, so cook it until it coats a spoon.
  • Grate the cheese finely so it melts evenly into the bechamel and browns nicely on top.

Variations

  • Vegetarian moussaka: replace the mince with lentils and mushrooms, keeping the same spices and bechamel.
  • With courgette: add a layer of roasted courgette slices with the aubergine, for extra vegetables.
  • Lighter version: roast the aubergine instead of frying it, and use semi skimmed milk for the bechamel.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the salting of aubergine. It stays bitter and soaks up oil, making the bake greasy.
  • Making a thin bechamel. It sinks and the top never sets golden.
  • Cutting too soon. The layers slide apart, so rest for at least 20 minutes.
  • Undercooking the mince sauce. A watery sauce makes the bake soggy, so simmer until thick.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make moussaka ahead?

Yes, and it is better made ahead. Assemble it, refrigerate for up to 1 day, and bake when needed, adding 10 minutes.

Can I use beef instead of lamb?

Yes. Beef makes a milder moussaka. Lamb is traditional and richer, so choose the one you prefer.

Is moussaka gluten free?

Not as written, because of the flour in the bechamel. Use a gluten free flour blend for the bechamel, and check your stock.

Why is my bechamel lumpy?

The milk was added too fast, or the flour was not cooked. Whisk constantly, add warm milk slowly, and cook until smooth.
