Spaghetti Carbonara

25 min · 4 people · World Food · Italy

Published on 28 October 2026

Real carbonara has no cream. It comes from Rome, and its sauce is made only from eggs, pecorino cheese, black pepper and the fat from guanciale or pancetta, mixed with hot pasta until it turns glossy and silky. The trick is heat control. The pan must be hot enough to cook the eggs gently into a sauce, but never so hot that they scramble. Work quickly, use pasta water to loosen the sauce, and serve at once. Once you learn the timing, this becomes one of the fastest and most comforting pasta dishes you can make, with only a handful of ingredients and a depth of flavour that tastes far more complex than it is.

Ingredients

  • Spaghetti 400g
  • Guanciale or pancetta 150g (diced)
  • Egg yolks 4
  • plus 1 whole egg
  • Pecorino Romano 60g (finely grated)
  • Black pepper 2 tsp (freshly cracked)
  • Salt for the pasta water
  • Extra pecorino
  • pepper to serve

Method

  1. Boil the spaghetti in well salted water until just al dente, and keep 1 cup of the pasta water before draining.
  2. Fry the guanciale or pancetta in a dry pan on medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes until crisp at the edges and the fat has melted out.
  3. Whisk the yolks, whole egg, pecorino and most of the black pepper into a thick paste. Add a spoon of pasta water if it is too stiff.
  4. Take the pan off the heat and let it cool for 1 minute, so the eggs will not scramble when they meet the pan.
  5. Add the drained spaghetti to the pan and toss well in the rendered fat for 30 seconds.
  6. Pour in the egg and cheese mix and toss fast and constantly, adding pasta water a splash at a time until the sauce is glossy and coats every strand.
  7. Taste, add more pepper, and add salt only if needed, because pecorino and pancetta are already salty.
  8. Serve at once in warm bowls, with extra pecorino and black pepper on top.

Chef's tip

Take the pan off the heat before adding the eggs. Residual heat makes the sauce, direct heat makes scrambled eggs.

Serving

Serves 4 as a main course, in warm bowls, with a simple green salad and crusty bread on the side. Carbonara is a one bowl meal, so keep the sides light and let the pasta be the star. Serve it the moment it is tossed, because the sauce thickens as it cools and loses its silkiness.

Storage and reheating

Carbonara is best eaten fresh and does not reheat well, because the egg sauce thickens and can split. If you must keep it, refrigerate for up to 1 day and reheat very gently in a pan with a splash of water, stirring constantly. Do not freeze it. Cooked pasta on its own keeps for 2 days, but make the sauce fresh.

Ingredient notes

  • Pecorino Romano is sharp and salty, and it is the traditional cheese. Parmesan works, but it is milder, so use a little more and taste before adding salt.
  • Guanciale is cured pork cheek and gives the classic flavour. Pancetta is the closest easy substitute, and bacon is acceptable, though smokier.
  • Pasta water is full of starch. It helps the egg sauce cling to the spaghetti instead of sliding off.
  • Freshly cracked black pepper matters here. It is a main flavour, not just a garnish, so crack it coarse and use plenty.

Variations

  • With peas: add a handful of peas to the pasta water in the last minute, for colour and sweetness.
  • Vegetarian style: replace the pork with mushrooms fried in butter and olive oil, and add a little smoked paprika, though the result is a different dish.
  • Lighter version: use 2 whole eggs and 2 yolks instead of 4 yolks, and a little less cheese, for a sauce that is still silky.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the eggs to a pan that is still on the heat. The eggs scramble and the sauce turns lumpy.
  • Using cream. It makes a different, heavier sauce and hides the pecorino flavour.
  • Draining all the pasta water. Without it, the sauce is too thick and will not coat the pasta.
  • Adding salt before tasting. Pecorino and cured pork are salty, so taste first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use bacon instead of guanciale?

Yes. Fry it until crisp, and drain a little of the fat if there is a lot. The flavour is smokier, but the method is the same.

Why did my sauce scramble?

The pan was too hot. Take it off the heat, cool it for a minute, and toss quickly with pasta water next time.

Can I make carbonara without pork?

Yes, with mushrooms or peas, but it will not taste like classic carbonara. Keep the egg and pecorino sauce the same.

How do I know the pasta water amount?

Start with a small splash and add more as you toss. The sauce should look glossy and loose, not thick and pasty.
