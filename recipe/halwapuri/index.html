Halwa Puri Nashta

50 min · 4 people · Breakfast · Pakistan

Published on 28 October 2026

Halwa puri is Sunday breakfast in Lahore, and in many homes it is a whole event, not just a meal. Three things arrive together, puffed puri, soft semolina halwa, and spiced chickpeas with potato, and the pleasure is in taking a little of each in one bite. Each part is simple on its own, but the timing matters. Fry the puri last, so it arrives puffed and hot, keep the halwa warm and soft, and let the chickpeas simmer until the masala coats them. Cook it on a slow morning, eat it together, and do not rush it.

Ingredients

  • Flour 2 cups
  • semolina 1 cup
  • Sugar 1 cup
  • ghee 1/2 cup
  • Chickpeas (boiled) 2 cups
  • Potato 1
  • onion
  • tomato
  • Garam masala
  • turmeric
  • red chilli
  • Cardamom
  • almonds for garnish

Method

  1. Knead the flour with water and a pinch of salt into a firm, smooth dough. Cover and rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Boil the chickpeas if not already boiled, until soft but not mushy, and keep 1 cup of their cooking water.
  3. Roast the semolina in ghee on low heat, stirring constantly, until it turns golden and smells nutty, about 8 to 10 minutes.
  4. Add hot water and sugar carefully, it will bubble, stir until the halwa thickens, then add cardamom and cover on low heat for 5 minutes.
  5. Heat oil, fry the onion until soft, add tomato, potato, garam masala, turmeric and red chilli, and fry the masala well.
  6. Add the chickpeas and their cooking water, simmer for 10 minutes, and mash a few chickpeas against the side to thicken the gravy.
  7. Roll small puris, no thicker than a coin edge, and deep fry in hot oil, pressing gently so each one puffs. Drain on paper.
  8. Serve puri, halwa and chickpeas together on one plate, with the halwa garnished with almonds.

Chef's tip

Do not use too much flour while rolling puri, or it will not puff. If the oil is well heated, the puri puffs at once, so test with a small piece of dough first, it should rise in seconds.

Serving

Serves 4, with 2 to 3 puris per person, a spoon of halwa, and a generous ladle of chickpeas on one plate. Serve with pickle, sliced onion and lemon, and chai on the side. Halwa puri is a sit down breakfast, eaten hot, with everyone at the table together. Make extra puri, because it disappears fastest.

Storage and reheating

Eat puri fresh, it does not keep well. Halwa keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days in an airtight container, and reheats in a pan on low heat with a splash of milk or water. Chickpeas keep for up to 3 days in the fridge, and reheat on the stove until bubbling. Do not store assembled plates, keep the three parts separate and combine at serving.

Ingredient notes

  • Firm dough makes puffed puri. Soft dough absorbs oil and stays flat, so knead it firm and rest it well.
  • Fine semolina gives a smooth halwa. Coarse semolina needs more water and longer cooking.
  • Keep the chickpea cooking water. It thickens the gravy naturally and carries flavour.
  • Cardamom goes into the halwa at the end, so its aroma stays fresh instead of cooking away.

Variations

  • Aloo ki tarkari instead of chickpeas: cook potatoes in the same masala, mash half of them, and serve as the savoury side.
  • Less sweet halwa: reduce the sugar to 3/4 cup and add a little extra cardamom, for a halwa that suits the spicy chickpeas better.
  • Whole wheat puri: use half whole wheat flour and half plain flour, rest the dough a little longer, and roll slightly thicker.

Common mistakes

  • Rolling puri too thick or with too much dry flour. It stays flat and chewy instead of puffing.
  • Roasting semolina on high heat. It browns in patches and tastes burnt, so keep the heat low and stir.
  • Adding cold water to hot semolina. It clumps, so use hot water and stir at once.
  • Frying puri in oil that is not hot enough. It soaks up oil and turns greasy.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my puri not puff?

The dough was too soft, the puri was rolled too thick, or the oil was not hot enough. Knead firm, roll thin, and test the oil first.

Can I use tinned chickpeas?

Yes. Drain and rinse them, then simmer in the masala for 10 minutes. Add a little hot water, because tinned chickpeas have no cooking water.

How sweet should the halwa be?

Traditional halwa puri halwa is quite sweet, to balance the spicy chickpeas. If you prefer less sweet, reduce the sugar, but do not cut it by half, or the texture changes.

Can I make parts ahead?

Yes. Make the halwa and chickpeas the day before and refrigerate. Fry the puri fresh on the morning, because reheated puri is never as good.
