Aussie Meat Pie

90 min · 6 people · World Food · Australia

The Aussie meat pie is an icon, handheld, golden and eaten at football games, bakeries and kitchen tables all over Australia. Inside a crisp shortcrust base and a flaky puff pastry lid is a rich beef filling, slow cooked with onion, tomato, Worcestershire sauce and a little Vegemite for depth, thick enough to hold its shape when the pie is cut, but juicy enough to need tomato sauce on top. Make the filling ahead and cool it fully, because warm filling melts the pastry, then bake until the lid is deep golden. Serve it hot, with tomato sauce, and eat it with your hands, the Australian way.

Ingredients

  • Beef chuck 700g (diced small)
  • Onion 1
  • garlic 2 cloves
  • Tomato paste 2 tbsp
  • beef stock 400ml
  • Worcestershire sauce 2 tbsp
  • Vegemite 1 tsp
  • Flour 2 tbsp
  • thyme 1 tsp
  • Shortcrust pastry for bases
  • puff pastry for lids
  • Egg 1 (beaten, for glazing)
  • Tomato sauce to serve
  • salt
  • pepper

Method

  1. Brown the beef in batches in a hot pot, then set aside. Brown the onion and garlic in the same pot.
  2. Add the tomato paste and flour, cook for 1 minute, then pour in the stock, stirring to lift the browned flavour.
  3. Return the beef, add Worcestershire, Vegemite and thyme, and simmer gently for 60 to 75 minutes until tender and thick.
  4. Cool the filling completely, in the fridge if possible. Warm filling will melt the pastry and make it soggy.
  5. Line greased pie tins or a muffin tin with shortcrust pastry, fill with the cold beef mixture, and brush the edges with egg.
  6. Top with puff pastry lids, press the edges to seal, trim, and cut a small steam hole in each lid.
  7. Brush with beaten egg, and bake at 200C for 25 to 30 minutes, until deep golden and crisp.
  8. Rest for 5 minutes, lift out of the tins, and serve hot with tomato sauce.

Chef's tip

Cool the filling completely before filling the pastry. Even slightly warm filling softens the pastry and stops it crisping in the oven.

Serving

Serves 6, as individual pies, hot from the oven, with tomato sauce on top and a simple salad or peas on the side. Meat pies are handheld food, so serve them on plates with a fork nearby, but encourage eating with hands while they are hot. They are perfect for lunch, for picnics, and for watching sport, with extra tomato sauce on the table.

Storage and reheating

Cooked pies keep in the fridge for up to 3 days, covered, and reheat in the oven at 180C until hot and crisp, about 15 minutes. They freeze well for up to 3 months, baked or unbaked, thawed overnight in the fridge. The filling on its own keeps for 3 days in the fridge and freezes for 3 months. Do not microwave pies if you want a crisp crust.

Ingredient notes

  • Beef chuck becomes tender and rich with slow cooking. Lean stewing beef dries out, and mince makes a different, softer pie.
  • Vegemite adds a savoury depth that is hard to place but unmistakably Australian. Use only a little, or it will overpower the filling.
  • Shortcrust for the base gives structure, puff pastry for the lid gives flake. Using puff pastry for both makes the base soggy.
  • Egg wash gives the golden shine. Brush it on just before baking, and do not let it pool in the steam hole.

Variations

  • With mushrooms: add sliced mushrooms with the onion, for extra flavour and a lighter filling.
  • Chicken pie: use chicken thighs with the same sauce, cooked for 40 minutes, for a chicken version.
  • Cheese and bacon pie: add grated cheese and cooked bacon to the cooled filling before baking.

Common mistakes

  • Filling the pastry while warm. The pastry melts and goes soggy, so cool the filling fully.
  • Underbaking the base. The bottom stays pale and soft, so bake until the lid is deep golden and the base is set.
  • Forgetting the steam hole. Steam builds up and bursts the lid, so cut a small hole in each pie.
  • Making the filling too thin. It runs out when cut, so simmer until thick enough to mound on a spoon.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use shop bought pastry?

Yes. Good quality shortcrust and puff pastry work well, and save a lot of time. Keep them cold until you use them.

Can I make one large pie instead?

Yes. Use a 20cm pie dish, line it with shortcrust, fill, top with puff pastry, and bake for 40 to 45 minutes.

What is Vegemite for?

It adds savoury, yeasty depth to the filling. If you cannot find it, use a little extra Worcestershire sauce and a pinch of salt.

Can I freeze meat pies?

Yes, baked or unbaked, for up to 3 months. Bake from frozen, adding 10 to 15 minutes, until hot through.
