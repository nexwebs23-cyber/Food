Seafood Paella

Jump to Recipe
Time55 min
Servings6 people
DifficultyMedium
CategoryWorld Food · Spain

Published on 28 October 2026

Paella comes from Valencia in Spain, where it is cooked outdoors in a wide shallow pan, and the rice, not the seafood, is the real star. Saffron gives it the golden colour, a good stock gives it flavour, and the last few minutes of cooking, left undisturbed, create socarrat, the crisp toasted layer of rice at the bottom that everyone fights over. Use short grain bomba rice if you can, spread it in an even layer, and resist stirring once the stock goes in. Serve it in the pan, in the middle of the table, with lemon wedges, and let everyone dig in together.

Ingredients and method

Ingredients

  • Bomba or short grain rice 400g
  • Prawns 300g
  • mussels 300g (cleaned)
  • Squid rings 200g (optional)
  • Chicken or fish stock 1 litre (hot)
  • Saffron 1 large pinch
  • Onion 1
  • red pepper 1
  • garlic 3 cloves
  • Tomatoes 2 (grated)
  • smoked paprika 1 tsp
  • Olive oil 4 tbsp
  • lemon 1
  • peas 100g

Method

  1. Soak the saffron in 3 tbsp of hot stock for 10 minutes, and keep the rest of the stock hot.
  2. Heat olive oil in a wide paella pan, fry the onion and pepper for 6 minutes until soft, then add garlic and paprika.
  3. Add the grated tomato and cook for 4 minutes until it darkens and thickens.
  4. Add the rice and stir for 1 minute to coat it in the sofrito, then spread it in an even layer.
  5. Pour in the hot stock and the saffron liquid, season with salt, and bring to a gentle boil. Do not stir from this point.
  6. Cook on medium heat for 15 minutes, then arrange the seafood and peas on top, pressing them lightly into the rice.
  7. Reduce the heat, cover loosely with foil, and cook for 10 minutes more, until the rice is tender and the mussels have opened.
  8. Rest the pan covered for 5 minutes, then serve in the pan with lemon wedges, listening for the crackle of the socarrat.

Chef's tip

Do not stir the rice after the stock goes in. Stirring releases starch and makes the rice sticky, and it stops the socarrat forming.

Good to know

Serving

Serves 6, served in the pan in the middle of the table, with lemon wedges and a green salad on the side. Each person takes a portion from the edge inwards, so everyone gets some of the crisp bottom layer. Paella is a sharing dish, so set out extra lemon, crusty bread and aioli if you like, and do not serve it on individual plates in the kitchen.

Storage and reheating

Paella is best eaten fresh, straight from the pan. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 2 days, and reheat in a hot pan with a splash of stock, covered, until steaming. The rice will not be as crisp, and the seafood should not be reheated for long. Do not freeze paella with shellfish, the texture suffers. The socarrat is only there on the day.

Ingredient notes

  • Bomba rice absorbs a lot of liquid and stays separate. Long grain rice goes mushy in paella, so use short grain rice if bomba is not available.
  • Keep the stock hot. Cold stock cools the pan and the rice cooks unevenly.
  • Smoked paprika gives a warm, smoky note. Sweet paprika works, but hot paprika can overpower the saffron.
  • Saffron should be soaked before use, so its colour and aroma spread evenly through the rice.

Variations

  • Chicken and chorizo paella: add browned chicken and sliced chorizo with the sofrito, and use chicken stock.
  • Vegetable paella: use vegetable stock, artichokes, green beans and butter beans, with the same saffron rice method.
  • Mixed paella: combine chicken, prawns and mussels, adding the chicken early and the seafood late, as in the main method.

Common mistakes

  • Stirring the rice. It turns sticky and the bottom never crisps.
  • Using the wrong rice. Long grain rice and risotto rice both give the wrong texture.
  • Adding cold stock. The cooking stops and starts, and the rice cooks unevenly.
  • Overloading the pan. Too much seafood on top steams the rice instead of letting it toast, so keep the layer even and not too deep.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a paella pan?

A wide shallow pan works, as long as the rice layer is thin. A deep pot makes the rice too thick and it steams instead of toasting.

Can I make paella without saffron?

You can, with a pinch of turmeric for colour, but the flavour is different. Saffron is the defining flavour of paella.

How do I know the rice is done?

Taste it. It should be tender with a slight bite, and the liquid should be absorbed. Resting for 5 minutes finishes it.

Why is my paella mushy?

The rice was stirred, the stock was cold, or the heat was too low. Keep the rice undisturbed and the heat steady.
