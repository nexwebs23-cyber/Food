Punjabi Samosa

45 min · 6 people · Street Food · India

Published on 28 October 2026

A good samosa is judged in the first bite, crisp pastry that shatters, then spiced potato with peas, ginger and coriander. The pastry must be firm and rested, the filling must be dry, not wet, and the frying must be slow, on low heat, so the crust turns golden all the way through instead of browning in a minute and staying pale inside. This recipe makes the classic Punjabi samosa, the one served with green and tamarind chutney and a cup of tea. Make a double batch, because they vanish fast, and freeze the extras before frying for another day.

Ingredients

  • Flour 2 cups
  • carom seeds 1/2 tsp
  • Potatoes (boiled) 4
  • peas 1/2 cup
  • Ginger
  • green chilli
  • garam masala
  • Fresh coriander
  • pomegranate seeds
  • Oil for frying

Nutrition (approx. per serving)

317 calories · Protein 7.4 g · Fat 7.6 g · Saturated fat 1 g · Carbs 54.1 g · Sugar 2.3 g · Fiber 4.5 g

Method

  1. Rub the carom seeds and 3 tbsp of oil into the flour with your fingertips until it looks like breadcrumbs.
  2. Add water a little at a time and knead a firm, stiff dough. It should not be soft. Cover and rest for 20 minutes.
  3. Boil the potatoes until just tender, cool them, and crumble them roughly. Do not mash them smooth.
  4. Heat 1 tbsp of oil, fry the ginger and green chilli for 30 seconds, add the peas, garam masala, salt and pomegranate seeds, and cook for 2 minutes.
  5. Add the potatoes and coriander, mix well, and cook for 2 to 3 minutes until the filling is dry. Cool it completely.
  6. Divide the dough into balls, roll each into an oval, cut in half, and form a cone, sealing the straight edge with water.
  7. Fill each cone three quarters full, seal the top edge firmly, and press to close. Check that no filling escapes.
  8. Fry in oil on low heat for 12 to 15 minutes, turning now and then, until deep golden and crisp. Drain and serve with chutneys.

Chef's tip

Always fry samosas on low heat. On high heat the pastry will burn and the inside will stay raw, and the crust will bubble instead of turning crisp and even.

Serving

Serves 6, with 2 samosas per person, green chutney, tamarind chutney, sliced onion and hot chai. Serve on a platter, cut one open to show the filling, and keep extra chutney on the side. Samosas are best hot, within 30 minutes of frying, while the pastry is still crisp. A little chaat masala sprinkled on top adds a tangy finish.

Storage and reheating

Eat fresh for the best crispness. Cooled, fried samosas keep in the fridge for up to 2 days and reheat in the oven at 180C for 8 to 10 minutes, until crisp again. Do not microwave, because the pastry turns soft. Freeze uncooked, shaped samosas on a tray, then bag them for up to 1 month, and fry from frozen on low heat, adding 3 to 4 minutes.

Ingredient notes

  • Firm dough is the secret to crisp pastry. Soft dough makes a chewy, oily samosa.
  • Cool the filling completely before filling. Warm filling steams the pastry from inside and weakens the seal.
  • Carom seeds give the classic flavour and help digestion. Do not skip them if you can find them.
  • Dry filling matters. Wet filling leaks, softens the pastry, and makes frying messy.

Variations

  • Keema samosa: replace the potato with cooked, spiced mince, cooled completely, and fry the same way.
  • Paneer samosa: use crumbled paneer with peas, garam masala and coriander, for a richer vegetarian filling.
  • Baked samosa: brush with oil and bake at 190C for 20 to 25 minutes, turning once. Less crisp, but lighter.

Common mistakes

  • Kneading a soft dough. It rolls easily, but fries soft and oily.
  • Filling with warm, wet filling. The seal opens and the filling leaks into the oil.
  • Frying on high heat. The outside browns fast and the pastry inside stays doughy.
  • Overfilling the cones. They burst in the oil, so fill only three quarters full.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my samosa pastry soft, not crisp?

The dough was too soft, or the frying heat was too high. Knead firm, rest the dough, and fry low and slow.

Can I make samosas in an air fryer?

Yes. Brush with oil and air fry at 180C for 12 to 15 minutes, turning once. The crust is crisp, though a little drier than deep fried.

How do I stop the samosa opening while frying?

Seal with water and press firmly, and let the shaped samosas rest for 10 minutes before frying, so the seal sets.

Can I use ready made pastry?

You can use spring roll sheets in a pinch, but the flavour and texture are different. The classic crisp comes from the firm flour dough in this recipe.
