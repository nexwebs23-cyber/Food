Falafel

40 min · 4 people · World Food · Middle East

Published on 28 October 2026

Real falafel is made from raw soaked chickpeas, never tinned or cooked chickpeas, and that is what gives it a light, fluffy inside and a crisp, craggy crust. The chickpeas are ground coarse with onion, garlic, parsley, coriander and cumin, rested so the mixture holds together, then shaped and fried until deep golden. Serve them in warm pitta with salad and tahini sauce, or as part of a mezze spread with hummus. The mixture can be made ahead and chilled, and the falafel fry in minutes, so they are perfect for a crowd, as long as you do not crowd the pan.

Ingredients

  • Dried chickpeas 250g (soaked overnight)
  • Onion 1
  • garlic 3 cloves
  • Parsley 1 large bunch
  • coriander 1 bunch
  • Cumin 2 tsp
  • coriander powder 1 tsp
  • Baking powder 1/2 tsp
  • flour 2 tbsp
  • Salt 1 tsp
  • black pepper
  • chilli flakes
  • Oil for frying
  • Pitta
  • salad
  • tahini sauce to serve

Method

  1. Soak the chickpeas in plenty of cold water overnight, then drain very well. Do not cook them.
  2. Put the chickpeas, onion, garlic, herbs, spices, salt and pepper in a food processor, and pulse until coarse and crumbly, not a paste.
  3. Transfer to a bowl, stir in the flour and baking powder, cover, and refrigerate for 30 minutes to firm up.
  4. Test one falafel first, shape a small ball, fry it, and check that it holds together and is cooked through.
  5. Shape the rest into small balls or patties, about the size of a walnut, with wet hands.
  6. Heat oil to 175C, and fry in batches for 3 to 4 minutes, turning once, until deep golden and crisp all over.
  7. Drain on a wire rack, not on paper, so the crust stays crisp, and sprinkle with a little salt.
  8. Serve hot in pitta with salad, tomato, cucumber and tahini sauce, or on a mezze plate with hummus.

Chef's tip

Use raw soaked chickpeas, never cooked chickpeas. Cooked chickpeas make a soft, mushy falafel that falls apart in the oil.

Serving

Serves 4, with 4 to 5 falafel per person, in warm pitta with salad and tahini, or as part of a mezze spread. Serve them the moment they come out of the oil, while the crust is crisp and the inside is hot and fluffy, with lemon wedges and extra tahini on the side. Falafel are finger food, so set out plenty of pitta, salad and sauces, and let everyone build their own.

Storage and reheating

Uncooked falafel mixture keeps in the fridge for up to 2 days, covered. Cooked falafel keep for 2 days in the fridge, and reheat in a hot oven until crisp, about 8 minutes. Do not microwave them, they go soft. Uncooked shaped falafel freeze well for 1 month, fried from frozen for a minute longer.

Ingredient notes

  • Soaking overnight is essential. Under soaked chickpeas stay hard inside, so give them a full 12 hours in cold water.
  • Pulse, do not puree. A coarse texture with visible bits of herb gives falafel its light, fluffy bite.
  • Chilling the mixture helps it hold its shape. If it feels wet, add a spoon of flour, but do not add too much or the falafel turn heavy.
  • Oil temperature matters. Too cool and the falafel soak up oil, too hot and they brown outside while staying raw inside.

Variations

  • Baked falafel: brush with oil and bake at 200C for 20 to 25 minutes, turning once, for a lighter version.
  • With broad beans: replace half the chickpeas with soaked split broad beans, for the Egyptian style taameya.
  • Spicy falafel: add 1 chopped fresh chilli and extra cumin to the mixture, and serve with chilli sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Using tinned chickpeas. They are already cooked, so the falafel turn mushy and fall apart.
  • Over processing the mixture. A smooth paste makes dense, heavy falafel, so pulse briefly and keep it coarse.
  • Frying in oil that is too cool. The falafel absorb oil and go greasy, so heat the oil properly first.
  • Shaping them too large. Large falafel stay raw inside, so keep them walnut sized.

Frequently asked questions

Can I bake falafel instead of frying?

Yes. They are less crisp, but still tasty. Brush with oil, bake at 200C, and turn once halfway.

Why did my falafel fall apart?

The mixture was too wet, or the oil was too cool. Chill the mixture, add a little flour, and test one first.

Can I use tinned chickpeas if I add flour?

It is not recommended. Even with flour, cooked chickpeas make a dense falafel. Soaked raw chickpeas are essential.

What sauce goes with falafel?

Tahini sauce thinned with lemon and water, plus salad in pitta. Chilli sauce and pickles are also traditional.
