Tacos al Pastor

40 min · 4 people · World Food · Mexico

Published on 28 October 2026

Tacos al pastor began with Lebanese shawarma cooking in Mexico City, and it became something entirely Mexican, pork marinated in dried chillies and achiote, cooked until charred at the edges, and served in warm corn tortillas with pineapple, onion and coriander. The marinade does the heavy work, so give it time, even 30 minutes makes a difference, and overnight is better. Cook the pork hot and fast so it chars without drying out, warm the tortillas over an open flame or in a dry pan, and keep the toppings simple. The sweet pineapple against the smoky, spiced pork is the whole point, so do not skip it, and serve with lime and salsa on the side.

Ingredients

  • Pork shoulder 700g (thinly sliced)
  • Dried guajillo chillies 3
  • ancho chilli 1
  • Achiote paste 2 tbsp
  • garlic 3 cloves
  • White vinegar 3 tbsp
  • orange juice 60ml
  • Cumin 1 tsp
  • oregano 1 tsp
  • salt
  • Pineapple 300g (cut into chunks)
  • Corn tortillas 12
  • white onion 1
  • coriander
  • Limes 2
  • salsa of your choice

Method

  1. Soak the dried chillies in hot water for 15 minutes until soft, then drain, keeping a little of the soaking water.
  2. Blend the chillies, achiote, garlic, vinegar, orange juice, cumin, oregano and salt into a smooth marinade, adding soaking water as needed.
  3. Coat the pork in the marinade, cover, and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, or overnight for the best flavour.
  4. Heat a large pan or griddle on high heat with a little oil, and cook the pork in batches for 3 to 4 minutes per side until charred and cooked through.
  5. In the same hot pan, sear the pineapple chunks for 1 to 2 minutes per side until golden at the edges.
  6. Chop the pork into small pieces and toss with the pineapple and a spoon of the pan juices.
  7. Warm the tortillas in a dry pan or over a flame until soft and lightly spotted, and keep them wrapped in a cloth.
  8. Fill each tortilla with pork and pineapple, top with diced onion and coriander, and serve with lime wedges and salsa.

Chef's tip

Cook in batches and do not crowd the pan. Crowded pork steams instead of charring, and you lose the crisp edges that make al pastor special.

Serving

Serves 4, with 3 tacos per person, plus lime, salsa, extra onion and coriander on the table. Serve the tacos as soon as they are filled, while the tortillas are warm and the pork is still sizzling, and let everyone add their own lime and salsa. A simple side of rice and beans, or a crisp cabbage salad, turns the tacos into a full meal without taking attention from them.

Storage and reheating

Marinated raw pork keeps in the fridge for up to 1 day. Cooked pork keeps for up to 3 days in the fridge, and reheats best in a hot pan with a splash of water, so it crisps again. Tortillas keep wrapped at room temperature for 1 day, or in the fridge for 3 days, and warm up in a dry pan. Do not store assembled tacos, they go soggy. Pineapple keeps separately for 2 days.

Ingredient notes

  • Achiote paste gives the red colour and earthy flavour. Find it in Mexican shops or online, and do not replace it with food colour alone.
  • Guajillo chillies are mild and fruity. Ancho adds depth. Remove the seeds if you want less heat, and wear gloves when handling dried chillies.
  • Pork shoulder stays juicy when cooked hot and fast. Lean pork loin dries out more easily, so slice it thinner and cook it for less time.
  • Corn tortillas are traditional. Flour tortillas work, but they are softer and less able to hold the juicy filling.

Variations

  • Chicken al pastor: use chicken thighs with the same marinade, and cook until charred, for a lighter version.
  • With avocado: add sliced avocado and a squeeze of lime for creaminess, keeping the pineapple as well.
  • Spicier version: add 1 chipotle in adobo to the marinade, and serve with a hot salsa on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Using a cool pan. The pork boils in its marinade instead of charring, so get the pan smoking hot first.
  • Skipping the marinade time. Even 30 minutes matters, and pork marinated for only 5 minutes tastes flat.
  • Overfilling the tortillas. They split and the filling falls out, so use 2 to 3 tbsp of pork per taco.
  • Forgetting the lime. The acid lifts the rich pork, so always serve lime wedges.

Frequently asked questions

Can I grill the pork instead?

Yes. Grill over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side, then chop and toss with pineapple. A griddle pan gives the closest result indoors.

What if I cannot find achiote paste?

Use 1 tbsp of smoked paprika, 1 tsp of ground cumin and a pinch of turmeric for colour. The flavour is different, but it still works.

Are corn tortillas gluten free?

Pure corn tortillas made only from corn, water and lime are naturally gluten free, but check the packet for cross contamination if that matters to you.

Can I make the marinade ahead?

Yes. Blend it and refrigerate for up to 3 days, or freeze it for 1 month. Marinate the pork on the day you cook it.
