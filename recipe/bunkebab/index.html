Bun Kebab

25 min · 4 people · Street Food · Pakistan

Bun kebab is Karachi street food with a loyal following, a soft bun, a fried shami kebab, sliced onion and tomato, and chutneys that bring heat, tang and freshness in every bite. It is fast food made at home in 25 minutes, provided the shami kebabs are ready, and it is best eaten hot, straight off the tawa, with the bun lightly toasted and still soft. The balance matters more than any single ingredient. Too much chutney drowns the kebab, too little leaves the bun dry, and a well toasted bun holds everything together without falling apart in your hands.

Ingredients

  • Shami kebabs 8, buns 4
  • Onion, tomato, green chutney
  • Tamarind chutney, yoghurt
  • Green chilli, fresh coriander
  • Butter for toasting the bun

Method

  1. Slice the onion into thin rings, slice the tomato, and keep the chutneys, chillies and coriander ready in small bowls.
  2. Heat a little oil on a tawa on medium heat. Fry the shami kebabs for 2 to 3 minutes per side, until hot through and lightly crisp at the edges.
  3. Remove the kebabs and keep them warm. Wipe the tawa lightly, add butter, and toast the cut sides of the buns until lightly golden, about 1 minute.
  4. Spread green chutney on the bottom bun and tamarind chutney on the top bun. A thin layer is enough, the kebab should still be the star.
  5. Place 2 kebabs on each bottom bun. If the kebabs are small, overlap them slightly so every bite has kebab.
  6. Add onion rings, tomato slices, green chilli and coriander. Add a spoon of whisked yoghurt if you like a cooler, creamier bite.
  7. Close the bun and press gently. The chutney should just reach the edges, not drip out.
  8. Serve hot at once, with extra chutney on the side and a cold drink. Eat with both hands, Karachi style.

Tip

Do not over-toast the bun. Lightly golden is enough, or it will become hard and crumbly, and the kebab will slide out instead of sitting snugly inside.

Serving

Serves 4, with 1 bun kebab per person, extra chutney, and a simple salad or fries on the side. Bun kebab is street food, so serve it wrapped in paper or on a plate, hot from the tawa, and eat it at once. It pairs well with a cold lassi, a fizzy drink, or chai. For a larger crowd, set out the buns, kebabs and chutneys and let people build their own.

Storage

Bun kebab is best eaten fresh and does not store well once assembled, because the bun soaks up the chutney. Keep the parts separate. Cooked shami kebabs keep in the fridge for up to 2 days and reheat on a tawa. Chutneys keep in the fridge for up to 4 days in sealed jars. Toast and assemble just before serving, so the bun stays soft and the kebab stays hot.

Frequently asked questions

What is a shami kebab?

A soft kebab made from minced meat and lentils, cooked, ground and shaped, then fried. It is soft enough to squash in a bun, unlike a firm seekh kebab.

Can I use frozen shami kebabs?

Yes. Fry them straight from frozen on medium heat, giving 1 to 2 extra minutes per side, until hot through. Do not thaw them first, because soft kebabs break.

Which bun is best?

A soft, plain burger bun. Sesame buns also work. Avoid hard rolls, because they crack and the filling falls out.

Can I make it without tamarind chutney?

Yes. Use extra green chutney and a squeeze of lemon for tang. The flavour is different, but it still works well.
