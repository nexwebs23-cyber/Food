Margherita Pizza

75 min · 4 people · World Food · Italy

Published on 28 October 2026

Margherita pizza is proof that simple food can be extraordinary. It was made famous in Naples, where a hot oven, a thin crust, bright tomato sauce and good mozzarella do all the work. The colours are said to echo the Italian flag, red tomato, white mozzarella and green basil, and the flavour is clean and fresh rather than heavy. This version uses a dough that rests long enough to become light and chewy, a sauce that is barely cooked, and a very hot oven so the crust blisters in minutes. Take time with the dough and the heat, and the pizza will taste like a pizzeria pizza, even from a home oven.

Ingredients

  • Strong bread flour 500g
  • warm water 325ml
  • Instant yeast 7g
  • fine salt 10g
  • olive oil 2 tbsp
  • San Marzano tomatoes 400g (crushed)
  • Fior di latte mozzarella 250g (torn)
  • Fresh basil leaves 1 handful
  • Garlic 1 clove
  • olive oil
  • salt for the sauce
  • Semolina or flour for dusting

Method

  1. Mix the flour, water, yeast and salt, knead for 8 to 10 minutes until smooth, then rest the dough covered for 1 hour, or overnight in the fridge for better flavour.
  2. Crush the tomatoes with garlic, a pinch of salt and 1 tbsp of olive oil. Do not cook the sauce, it cooks on the pizza.
  3. Divide the dough into 4 balls, rest for 20 minutes, then stretch each one by hand from the centre out, leaving a thicker edge.
  4. Heat the oven as hot as it will go, at least 250C, with a pizza stone or an upside down baking tray inside for 30 minutes.
  5. Spread 3 to 4 tbsp of sauce on each base, leaving the edge clear, and scatter the torn mozzarella over the top.
  6. Bake for 6 to 8 minutes until the crust is blistered and golden and the cheese is bubbling.
  7. Add the basil leaves as soon as the pizza comes out, so they wilt gently without burning.
  8. Drizzle with a little olive oil, slice, and serve at once while the crust is crisp.

Chef's tip

Do not overload the pizza. A thin layer of sauce and a modest amount of cheese let the crust cook through and stay crisp.

Serving

Serves 4, with one pizza per person, or cut into slices for sharing with a green salad. Serve straight from the oven, with extra basil, chilli oil or parmesan on the table, and let everyone add their own. Pizza is at its best in the first 10 minutes, when the base is crisp and the cheese is still soft and stretchy.

Storage and reheating

Leftover slices keep in the fridge for up to 2 days in an airtight container. Reheat in a hot oven or a dry frying pan with a lid, so the base crisps again. Avoid the microwave, it makes the crust soft and chewy. Raw dough keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days, and freezes well for up to 1 month, thawed overnight in the fridge.

Ingredient notes

  • Bread flour gives the chew that plain flour cannot, so use it if you can find it, and weigh the flour for a reliable dough.
  • Fior di latte is the soft mozzarella sold in water. Drain and tear it, and pat it dry, so the pizza does not turn wet.
  • San Marzano tomatoes are sweet and low in acid. If you use another tin, crush them well and add a pinch of sugar only if they taste sharp.
  • Semolina on the peel stops the dough sticking and adds a little crunch. Flour alone can burn and taste bitter at high heat.

Variations

  • Marinara: skip the cheese, add extra garlic and oregano, and finish with olive oil, for a dairy free pizza.
  • Four cheese: replace the tomato sauce with a thin layer of olive oil, then use mozzarella, gorgonzola, fontina and parmesan.
  • With cherry tomatoes: roast halved cherry tomatoes with olive oil first, then use them instead of the crushed tomato sauce.

Common mistakes

  • Using a cool oven. The crust steams instead of blistering, so preheat the stone or tray fully.
  • Rolling the dough with a rolling pin. It presses out the air, so stretch it by hand instead.
  • Adding too much sauce or cheese. The centre stays wet and the base never crisps.
  • Putting basil on before baking at very high heat. It blackens, so add it after baking.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my pizza base soggy in the middle?

The oven or stone was not hot enough, or there was too much sauce and cheese. Preheat fully, use less topping, and bake on the lowest shelf.

Can I make the dough ahead?

Yes. After kneading, refrigerate it for up to 3 days. Bring it back to room temperature for 30 to 45 minutes before stretching.

Do I need a pizza stone?

No. An upside down heavy baking tray, preheated in the oven, works well. A cast iron pan also gives a crisp base.

Can I use dried basil?

Fresh basil is much better here, because it is the main flavour. Dried basil in the sauce is fine, but add fresh leaves to finish.
