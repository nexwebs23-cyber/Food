Chicken Karahi

40 min · 4 people · Desi Dishes · Pakistan

Chicken karahi is dhaba food at its best, cooked fast in a deep wok, with no onion and no long list of spices to hide behind. Fresh tomatoes break down into the masala, ginger and green chillies go in at the end, and the whole dish is ready in about 40 minutes. The trick is heat and timing. Keep the flame high, let the tomatoes do their work, and finish with ginger while the karahi is still sizzling. Eat it straight from the kadhai with hot roti or naan, while the masala is glossy and the chicken is tender.

Ingredients

  • Chicken 750g, tomatoes 4 large
  • Ginger-garlic paste 1 tbsp
  • Green chillies 4, ginger julienne
  • Ghee / oil 1/2 cup
  • Salt, crushed red chilli, garam masala
  • Fresh coriander

Method

  1. Cut the tomatoes into quarters and keep the ginger, chillies and coriander ready. Karahi moves fast, so everything must be within reach.
  2. Heat the ghee in a kadhai on high heat. Add the chicken and fry for 4 to 5 minutes, turning often, until the outside changes colour.
  3. Add the ginger-garlic paste and fry for 1 minute. Add the tomatoes and a little salt, cover, and cook for 8 to 10 minutes on medium high heat.
  4. Uncover and mash the softened tomatoes with the back of a spoon. Keep frying until the masala thickens and the oil rises to the top.
  5. Add the crushed red chilli and taste for salt. If the masala looks dry, add 2 tbsp of hot water only, no more.
  6. Add half the ginger julienne and two sliced green chillies. Fry for 2 more minutes so their flavour goes into the masala.
  7. Sprinkle garam masala, cover, and give 5 minutes of dum on low heat. The chicken should be cooked through and still juicy.
  8. Top with the remaining ginger, chillies and fresh coriander. Serve at once, straight from the kadhai, with roti or naan.

Tip

Do not add water to karahi. The tomatoes own water is enough. Keep the heat high so the chicken stays tender and the masala is well fried, and add the fresh ginger at the end, not at the start.

Serving

Serves 4 with roti or naan, a simple salad of onion and lemon, and raita if you like it. Serve in the kadhai itself, with lemon wedges on the side, and eat while it is hot. The masala is meant to be scooped up with bread, so keep extra roti ready. A cold drink or lassi helps with the heat.

Storage

Cool within 1 hour and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Reheat in a kadhai on medium heat, covered, with 1 to 2 tbsp of water, until steaming hot. Add fresh ginger and coriander after reheating. Freezing is possible for up to 1 month, but the tomato masala softens on thawing, so fresh is best.

Notes

['Use ripe, soft tomatoes. Hard tomatoes take longer to break down and leave the masala watery.', 'Bone-in chicken gives more flavour and stays juicy. Boneless thigh also works, but cook it for 3 to 4 minutes less.', 'Ghee gives the dhaba flavour. Oil works, but use a little extra ginger at the end to lift it.', 'Fresh ginger julienne is a garnish and a flavour. Dried ginger powder is not a substitute here.']

Variations

['Mutton karahi: use 750g mutton, cook covered for 40 to 50 minutes until tender, then fry the masala on high heat as usual.', 'Green chilli karahi: skip red chilli, use 8 green chillies, half in the masala and half as garnish, for a fresh, sharp heat.', 'Butter karahi: finish with 1 tbsp of butter and 2 tbsp of cream, stirred in off the heat, for a richer, milder version.']

Common mistakes

['Adding water at the start. The masala turns into a thin curry instead of a thick, glossy karahi.', 'Cooking on low heat throughout. The chicken steams, the tomatoes stay chunky, and the masala never fries properly.', 'Adding ginger at the start only. It cooks away, and the fresh finish is lost.', 'Overcooking boneless chicken. It dries out quickly at high heat, so check it early.']

Frequently asked questions

Why does my karahi have no masala?

The tomatoes were not mashed and fried long enough, or water was added. Mash the tomatoes well and fry until the oil separates.

Can I make karahi without a kadhai?

Yes. Use a deep, heavy wok or frying pan. A thin pan burns the masala, so keep the heat a little lower and stir more.

Is karahi supposed to have onion?

Traditional Pakistani karahi has no onion. The tomato water makes the masala. Onion makes a different dish, closer to a curry.

How do I know the chicken is cooked?

The juices run clear when a piece is cut at the thickest part, and the meat is no longer pink. If unsure, cut the largest piece to check.
