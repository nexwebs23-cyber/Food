Pad Thai

30 min · 4 people · World Food · Thailand

Published on 28 October 2026

Pad thai is Thailand's most famous noodle dish, and its magic is balance, sweet, sour, salty and a little heat, all in one hot wok. The sauce is made from tamarind, fish sauce, palm sugar and chilli, and it coats rice noodles that are soaked, not boiled, so they stay springy instead of soft. Prawns, egg, bean sprouts and chives go in quickly, at high heat, and the dish is finished with crushed peanuts, lime and extra sprouts on the side. Have everything ready before you start, because once the wok is hot, the cooking takes only a few minutes, and that speed is what keeps the noodles from turning mushy.

Ingredients

  • Flat rice noodles 250g
  • Prawns 300g (peeled)
  • Eggs 2 (lightly beaten)
  • Tamarind paste 3 tbsp
  • fish sauce 3 tbsp
  • Palm sugar or brown sugar 3 tbsp
  • Garlic 3 cloves
  • chilli flakes 1 tsp
  • Bean sprouts 2 cups
  • garlic chives or spring onions
  • Roasted peanuts 4 tbsp (crushed)
  • lime 1

Method

  1. Soak the rice noodles in warm water for 20 to 30 minutes until pliable but still firm, then drain well.
  2. Mix the tamarind, fish sauce, sugar and chilli flakes into a sauce, and taste it. It should be sweet, sour and salty at once.
  3. Heat oil in a wok on high heat, fry the garlic for 20 seconds, then add the prawns and cook for 2 minutes until just pink.
  4. Push the prawns to one side, pour in the eggs, and scramble them gently until just set, then mix with the prawns.
  5. Add the drained noodles and the sauce, and toss constantly for 2 to 3 minutes until the noodles are tender and the sauce is absorbed.
  6. Add half the bean sprouts and the chives, toss for 30 seconds, and take the wok off the heat.
  7. Divide between plates, top with the remaining sprouts, crushed peanuts and spring onions.
  8. Serve at once with lime wedges, extra chilli flakes and fish sauce on the side, for each person to adjust.

Chef's tip

Soak the noodles, do not boil them. Boiled noodles overcook in the wok and turn soft and sticky.

Serving

Serves 4, on warm plates, with lime wedges, crushed peanuts, extra bean sprouts and chilli flakes on the table. Pad thai is a street food dish, so serve it hot and casual, with everyone squeezing lime over their own plate and adding more peanuts or heat as they like. A light cucumber salad on the side is refreshing, but keep the focus on the noodles.

Storage and reheating

Pad thai is best eaten fresh, straight from the wok. Leftovers keep in the fridge for up to 1 day, and reheat in a hot wok or pan with a splash of water, tossing constantly. The noodles soften on reheating, so do not expect the same texture. Do not freeze it. The sauce on its own keeps in the fridge for up to 1 week.

Ingredient notes

  • Tamarind paste gives the sour note that defines pad thai. If it is very thick, thin it with a little warm water before mixing the sauce.
  • Palm sugar melts into a caramel like sweetness. Brown sugar works well, but white sugar is sharper, so use a little less.
  • Fish sauce is salty and pungent. Add it to the sauce, not directly to the wok, so it blends evenly.
  • Flat rice noodles vary in thickness. Thinner noodles soak faster, so check them after 15 minutes and drain while still firm.

Variations

  • Chicken pad thai: replace the prawns with thinly sliced chicken, cooked through before the eggs go in.
  • Tofu pad thai: use firm tofu, fried until golden first, for a vegetarian version, and check that your fish sauce substitute suits you.
  • Extra vegetables: add shredded carrot and shredded cabbage with the noodles, for more crunch and colour.

Common mistakes

  • Overcooking the noodles. They should be firm when drained, because they finish cooking in the sauce.
  • Using a wok that is not hot enough. The noodles steam and stick, so heat the wok until it just starts to smoke.
  • Adding all the sprouts too early. They wilt and go soft, so add half at the end and keep half raw for topping.
  • Forgetting to taste the sauce. Balance it before cooking, because you cannot fix sweet, sour and salty once the noodles are in.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make pad thai without fish sauce?

Yes, use soy sauce with a pinch of salt, or a vegetarian fish sauce. The flavour is different, but the balance of sweet and sour stays.

What if my noodles stick together?

They were soaked too long or the wok was not hot enough. Drain well, toss with a little oil, and cook in a hotter wok next time.

Can I use dried shrimp?

Yes. Soak 2 tbsp of dried shrimp and add them with the garlic. They add a traditional salty depth.

Is pad thai gluten free?

Rice noodles are naturally gluten free, but fish sauce and tamarind paste can contain additives, so check the labels.
