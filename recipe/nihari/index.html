Beef Nihari

90 min · 5 people · Desi Dishes · Pakistan

Nihari is slow food in the truest sense, a weekend stew that rewards patience with meat so tender it leaves the bone and a gravy that coats the spoon. In this version beef shank cooks gently for about two hours until the connective tissue softens and the masala deepens in colour and flavour. The flour slurry at the end is what gives nihari its famous thick, glossy finish, so it must go in slowly and be stirred well. Serve it bubbling hot with fresh naan and the classic garnish, and keep extra lemon on the table.

Ingredients

  • Beef (shank) 1 kg
  • Nihari masala 4 tbsp
  • flour 3 tbsp
  • Ginger-garlic paste 2 tbsp
  • onion 1
  • Ghee / oil 1/2 cup
  • Ginger
  • green chilli
  • lemon (for garnish)
  • Fresh coriander

Method

  1. Heat the ghee, lightly brown the sliced onion without burning it, add the ginger-garlic paste, and fry for 1 minute until the raw smell goes.
  2. Add the beef and the nihari masala and fry on medium high heat for 8 to 10 minutes, until the meat changes colour and the masala coats every piece.
  3. Add enough hot water to cover the meat by about 2 inches, bring it to a boil, and skim off any foam.
  4. Cover the pot and cook on low heat for 2 hours. Stir now and then, and add hot water if the level drops below the meat.
  5. Check the meat. It should shred with a fork and leave the bone easily. If it is still firm, cook for 20 to 30 minutes more.
  6. Mix the flour with 1 cup of cool water until completely smooth, with no lumps. Pour it in a thin stream while stirring the pot.
  7. Simmer for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring often, until the gravy is thick and glossy and coats the back of a spoon.
  8. Rest for 10 minutes, taste for salt, and serve with naan, garnished with ginger, green chilli, lemon and coriander.

Chef's tip

Do not keep the gravy thin. Nihari is known for its thick, glossy gravy. Add the flour slowly so no lumps form, and stir constantly while it goes in.

Serving

Serves 5 with naan and small bowls of garnish on the table, so each person can add ginger, chilli, lemon and coriander to taste. Nihari is traditionally a breakfast or weekend lunch dish, served in deep bowls with plenty of gravy spooned over the meat. Keep the naan warm and bring the pot to the table while it is still bubbling.

Storage and reheating

Cool and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The gravy thickens as it cools. Reheat gently on low heat with a splash of hot water, stirring until steaming hot throughout. Freeze for up to 2 months, thaw overnight in the fridge, and add the garnish fresh at serving only.

Ingredient notes

  • Shank, with bone and connective tissue, is ideal for nihari. It gives body to the gravy. Boneless stewing beef works, but the gravy is less rich.
  • Nihari masala varies in strength by brand. Use the stated amount the first time, and adjust next time.
  • Ghee gives the traditional flavour. Oil works too, and half ghee and half oil is a good middle way.
  • Flour is there for thickness only. Do not add extra, because it dulls the spice.

Variations

  • Mutton nihari: use the same method and cook for about 1 hour and 30 minutes, checking earlier, because mutton softens sooner.
  • Chicken nihari: use bone-in chicken, cook for 35 to 40 minutes, add the flour slurry sooner, and expect a lighter gravy.
  • Spicier: add extra green chillies at serving, not during cooking, so the heat stays fresh.

Common mistakes

  • Cooking on high heat. The meat toughens and the gravy reduces too fast.
  • Adding the flour dry or all at once. Lumps form and cannot be stirred out easily.
  • Skimping on cooking time. The meat stays chewy instead of falling off the bone.
  • Serving with cold naan and no garnish. The flavour feels flat without them.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my nihari gravy thin?

The flour was too little, or the simmer after adding it was too short. Simmer for 10 to 15 minutes while stirring. If needed, add 1 extra tbsp of flour dissolved in water.

Can I use a pressure cooker?

Yes. Cook the meat and masala for 25 to 30 minutes after pressure is reached, then finish in an open pot with the flour slurry. Follow your cooker instructions for safety.

Is nihari very spicy?

The masala gives warmth, not burning heat. Fresh chillies at serving let each person choose how hot their bowl is.

Can I make it a day ahead?

Yes. The flavour improves overnight in the fridge. Reheat gently, add a little hot water if it is too thick, and add the garnish fresh.
