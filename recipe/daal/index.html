Daal Fry (Tadka Daal)

35 min · 4 people · Desi Dishes · India

Daal fry is the everyday dish that proves simple food can still be memorable, provided the daal is cooked soft and the tadka is poured on hot and covered at once. Masoor and moong cook quickly and turn creamy without any cream, and the ghee, garlic, cumin and whole red chillies on top give the aroma that reaches the table before the bowl does. This is comfort food for rice or roti, for busy weeknights and for days when nothing fancy is needed. The whole recipe takes about 35 minutes, and most of that is the daal quietly boiling.

Ingredients

  • Masoor / moong daal 1½ cups
  • Turmeric 1/2 tsp, salt
  • Garlic 4 cloves, cumin 1 tsp
  • Whole red chillies 3, ghee 3 tbsp
  • Green chilli, fresh coriander, lemon

Method

  1. Rinse the daal in several changes of water until the water is mostly clear, then drain it well.
  2. Put the daal in a pot with turmeric and 4 cups of water. Bring it to a boil, skim off the foam, and reduce the heat.
  3. Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring now and then, until the daal is completely soft and creamy. Add hot water if it gets too thick.
  4. Taste and add salt. The daal should be pourable, not stiff, because it thickens as it cools.
  5. Slice the garlic thinly. Heat the ghee in a small pan on medium heat and add the cumin. When it sizzles, add the garlic.
  6. Fry until the garlic is golden, not brown. Add the whole red chillies, count to ten, and take the pan off the heat at once.
  7. Pour the hot tadka over the daal in one go, listen for the sizzle, and cover the pot for 2 minutes so the aroma stays in.
  8. Stir in the green chilli and coriander, squeeze lemon over the top, and serve hot with rice or roti.

Tip

Cover the daal as soon as you pour the tadka. This keeps the aroma inside the daal instead of letting it escape into the kitchen, and it lets the garlic flavour spread through the whole pot.

Serving

Serves 4 with steamed rice or roti, a wedge of lemon, and a simple salad or pickle on the side. Daal is best served loose and hot, in deep bowls, with the tadka visible on top. If serving with rice, spoon the daal over the rice at the table. A fried egg or a piece of papad turns it into a fuller meal.

Storage

Cool and refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The daal thickens in the fridge, so stir in a splash of hot water when reheating. Reheat on low heat, stirring, until steaming hot, and add fresh coriander after reheating. Freeze for up to 1 month in portions, thaw in the fridge, and make a fresh tadka for the best aroma.

Frequently asked questions

Which daal is best for tadka daal?

Masoor is quickest and creamiest. Moong is a close second. Toor daal also works, but it needs longer cooking and a pressure cooker helps.

Can I make daal fry without ghee?

Yes. Use oil and add an extra clove of garlic. The result is lighter, and the tadka still works, but the aroma is less rich.

Why is my daal watery?

It was not simmered long enough, or too much water was used. Simmer uncovered for a few minutes to thicken it, and remember it thickens more as it cools.

Can I cook the daal in a pressure cooker?

Yes. Cook for 2 whistles or about 8 minutes at pressure, let the pressure release naturally, then finish with the tadka in an open pot.
