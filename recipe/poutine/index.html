Poutine

45 min · 4 people · World Food · Canada

Published on 28 October 2026

Poutine comes from Quebec in Canada, and it is gloriously simple, hot crisp fries, fresh cheese curds that squeak when you bite them, and a rich brown gravy poured over the top so the curds just start to melt. The fries must be crisp enough to hold the gravy without going soggy in a minute, so double frying is worth it, and the gravy must be piping hot, because it is the heat that softens the curds. Serve it at once, in a bowl, with a fork, and eat it while the fries are still crisp at the edges and the gravy is pooling at the bottom.

Ingredients

  • Potatoes 1kg (floury variety)
  • Cheese curds 250g (at room temperature)
  • Beef or vegetable stock 500ml
  • Butter 2 tbsp
  • flour 2 tbsp
  • Soy sauce 1 tsp
  • Worcestershire sauce 1 tsp
  • Garlic 1 clove
  • black pepper
  • Oil for deep frying
  • salt
  • Spring onions to serve (optional)

Method

  1. Cut the potatoes into thick batons, soak in cold water for 30 minutes, then drain and dry very well.
  2. Heat oil to 160C, fry the chips in batches for 5 to 6 minutes until pale and soft, then drain and rest for 10 minutes.
  3. While the chips rest, melt butter in a pan, stir in the flour, and cook for 2 minutes until lightly golden.
  4. Whisk in the hot stock a little at a time, then add soy, Worcestershire, garlic and pepper, and simmer for 5 minutes until smooth and glossy.
  5. Heat the oil to 190C, and fry the chips again in batches for 2 to 3 minutes until deep golden and very crisp. Drain and salt at once.
  6. Pile the hot chips into warm bowls, scatter the cheese curds over the top, and keep the curds at room temperature so they soften, not melt away.
  7. Pour the piping hot gravy over the top, so it runs into the gaps and starts to melt the curds.
  8. Serve at once with a fork, with spring onions scattered over if you like, and eat while hot.

Chef's tip

Use cheese curds at room temperature. Cold curds stay firm and do not soften, while room temperature curds go soft and squeaky under the hot gravy.

Serving

Serves 4, as a snack, a side, or a sharing main course, in deep bowls with forks. Serve it the moment the gravy is poured, while the chips are crisp and the curds are softening, and do not let it sit, because the chips soften quickly. Poutine is casual food, so serve it in the middle of the table, with extra gravy in a jug for anyone who wants more.

Storage and reheating

Poutine is best eaten fresh and does not reheat well once assembled, because the chips go soft. Store the components separately, chips, curds and gravy, for up to 2 days in the fridge. Reheat the chips in a hot oven until crisp, reheat the gravy until boiling, and assemble fresh. Gravy on its own freezes well for 1 month.

Ingredient notes

  • Floury potatoes make the fluffiest chips. Waxy potatoes stay dense and do not crisp in the same way.
  • Cheese curds should be fresh and squeaky. If you cannot find curds, use torn pieces of fresh mozzarella, though the squeak will be missing.
  • Gravy should be thin enough to run, not thick like a stew. It thickens as it cools, so serve it very hot.
  • Double frying is the secret to crisp chips that last. The first fry cooks the inside, the second fry crisps the outside.

Variations

  • With pulled pork: top with slow cooked pulled pork and extra gravy, for a heartier main course.
  • With mushrooms: add fried mushrooms and thyme to the gravy, and use vegetable stock, for a vegetarian version.
  • Breakfast poutine: add a fried egg on top, and serve with bacon on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Using cold cheese curds. They stay hard and do not melt into the fries.
  • Pouring lukewarm gravy. It does not soften the curds, so heat the gravy until it is steaming hot.
  • Frying the chips only once. They go soft under the gravy in minutes, so double fry them.
  • Salting the chips before the second fry. Salt draws out water and stops them crisping, so salt only at the end.

Frequently asked questions

What are cheese curds?

Fresh pieces of curdled milk, pressed lightly, with a squeaky texture when bitten. They are the defining ingredient of poutine.

Can I use frozen chips?

Yes. Cook them until very crisp in the oven or air fryer, then assemble with hot gravy and curds. Double frying fresh chips is better, but frozen chips work.

Is poutine vegetarian?

With vegetable stock and vegetarian Worcestershire sauce it can be. Traditional gravy uses beef stock, so check the stock you use.

Can I make the gravy ahead?

Yes. Make it up to 3 days ahead, refrigerate, and reheat until boiling before pouring over the chips.
