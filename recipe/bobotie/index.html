Bobotie

75 min · 6 people · World Food · South Africa

Published on 28 October 2026

Bobotie is South Africa's beloved baked mince dish, with roots in Cape Malay cooking, where spiced mince is sweetened with fruit, enriched with soaked bread, and baked under a golden egg custard topping with bay leaves. The flavour is warm and gently sweet, curry powder, turmeric, chutney, raisins and apricot all working together, and the texture is soft and comforting, somewhere between a savoury bake and a light meatloaf. Serve it with yellow rice, chutney and a simple salad, and let the sweet and savoury balance do the talking. It is an ideal make ahead dish for a crowd, because it reheats beautifully and tastes even better the next day.

Ingredients

  • Beef or lamb mince 750g
  • Onion 1
  • garlic 2 cloves
  • ginger 1 tsp
  • Curry powder 2 tbsp
  • turmeric 1 tsp
  • White bread 2 slices
  • milk 250ml
  • Eggs 3
  • bay leaves 4
  • Chutney 3 tbsp
  • raisins 3 tbsp
  • Dried apricots 4 (chopped)
  • lemon juice 1 tbsp
  • Almonds 2 tbsp (flaked)
  • salt
  • pepper

Method

  1. Soak the bread in 125ml of the milk until soft, then squeeze out the excess milk and crumble the bread.
  2. Fry the onion, garlic and ginger in oil for 5 minutes until soft, then add the curry powder and turmeric and fry for 1 minute.
  3. Add the mince and fry, breaking it up, until browned all over and the liquid has cooked off.
  4. Stir in the soaked bread, chutney, raisins, apricots, lemon juice and seasoning, and cook for 5 minutes. Taste and adjust the sweet and sour balance.
  5. Spoon the mince into a buttered baking dish, press it down evenly, and tuck the bay leaves into the top.
  6. Whisk the remaining milk with 2 eggs and a pinch of salt, and pour it gently over the mince.
  7. Bake at 180C for 35 to 40 minutes, until the topping is set and golden, and the edges are bubbling.
  8. Rest for 10 minutes, scatter with flaked almonds, and serve with yellow rice and extra chutney.

Chef's tip

Taste the mince before baking. The sweet, sour and spicy balance must be right at this stage, because baking will not fix it.

Serving

Serves 6, with yellow rice, fruit chutney and a crisp green salad or sliced tomato and onion salad. Serve it straight from the baking dish, in generous squares, with the golden topping and a bay leaf on each portion. Bobotie is a family dish, so put the dish in the middle of the table, with the rice and chutney alongside, and let everyone help themselves.

Storage and reheating

Bobotie keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days, covered, and reheats well in the oven at 160C until hot through. It also freezes well for up to 3 months, thawed overnight in the fridge. The egg topping can weep a little on reheating, so reheat gently and covered with foil. Yellow rice keeps separately for 2 days.

Ingredient notes

  • Soaked bread keeps the mince soft and light. Without it, bobotie can turn dense and dry.
  • Curry powder varies a lot. Use a mild Cape Malay style curry powder if you can, and add more only after tasting.
  • Chutney adds sweetness and tang. Fruit chutney is traditional, and mango chutney works well too.
  • Bay leaves go on top, not mixed in, so they flavour the custard and look beautiful when served.

Variations

  • With lentils: replace half the mince with cooked brown lentils, for a lighter version with the same spices.
  • Vegetarian bobotie: use lentils and mushrooms with the same fruit, chutney and egg topping.
  • Spicier version: add 1 chopped fresh chilli with the onion, and serve with extra chutney and sambal on the side.

Common mistakes

  • Using very lean mince without bread. It turns dry and crumbly, so keep the soaked bread in.
  • Overbaking the topping. It goes rubbery, so take it out as soon as it is set and golden.
  • Skipping the rest. The bobotie falls apart when cut hot, so rest for 10 minutes before serving.
  • Making it too sweet. Taste the mince, and balance chutney and fruit with lemon juice, not more sugar.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make bobotie ahead?

Yes. Assemble it fully, refrigerate for up to 1 day, and bake when needed, adding 10 minutes to the baking time.

Is bobotie spicy?

It is warmly spiced, not hot. The curry powder gives flavour, and the fruit and chutney keep it gentle.

What is yellow rice?

Rice cooked with turmeric, raisins and sometimes cinnamon, served with bobotie in South Africa. Plain rice also works.

Can I use lamb mince?

Yes. Lamb makes a richer bobotie. Drain any excess fat after browning, so the bake is not greasy.
