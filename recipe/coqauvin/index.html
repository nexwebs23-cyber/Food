Coq au Vin

120 min · 4 people · World Food · France

Published on 28 October 2026

Coq au vin is classic French comfort food, chicken braised slowly in red wine until it falls from the bone, with mushrooms, pearl onions and bacon in a sauce that tastes of the wine without tasting alcoholic. It comes from Burgundy, where the wine is part of the cooking, not just an accompaniment, and the long gentle braise is what makes the chicken tender and the sauce rich. Brown the chicken well first, because that colour becomes flavour, then let the pot do the work in the oven. Serve it with mashed potatoes or crusty bread, and give the sauce a final taste for salt and a little butter to make it shine.

Ingredients

  • Chicken thighs
  • legs 1kg (bone in)
  • Red wine 500ml (Burgundy style if possible)
  • Bacon lardons 150g
  • Mushrooms 250g
  • pearl onions 12
  • Carrot 1
  • garlic 3 cloves
  • thyme 3 sprigs
  • Chicken stock 250ml
  • tomato paste 1 tbsp
  • Flour 2 tbsp
  • butter 2 tbsp
  • Parsley to serve
  • salt
  • pepper

Method

  1. Season the chicken well, dust lightly with flour, and brown it in batches in a hot ovenproof pot until deep golden on both sides. Set aside.
  2. Fry the bacon in the same pot until crisp, lift it out, and brown the mushrooms and pearl onions in the bacon fat.
  3. Add the carrot, garlic and tomato paste, and cook for 2 minutes, stirring, until fragrant.
  4. Pour in the wine, scraping the bottom of the pot to lift all the browned flavour, and simmer for 3 minutes.
  5. Return the chicken and bacon to the pot, add stock and thyme, and bring to a gentle simmer. The liquid should come halfway up the chicken.
  6. Cover and cook in the oven at 160C for 75 to 90 minutes, until the chicken is very tender.
  7. Lift out the chicken, and simmer the sauce on the stove for 10 minutes until it coats a spoon. Whisk in the butter at the end.
  8. Return the chicken to the sauce, warm through, and serve scattered with parsley.

Chef's tip

Brown the chicken properly before braising. Pale chicken makes a pale sauce, while deep golden chicken makes a rich, full flavoured sauce.

Serving

Serves 4, in shallow bowls, with creamy mashed potatoes, buttered noodles or crusty bread to catch the sauce. The chicken should be served with a generous ladle of sauce, mushrooms and onions, and a scattering of parsley. This is a Sunday dish, so serve it at the table from the pot, with a simple green salad on the side and the rest of the wine, if you like, in glasses.

Storage and reheating

Coq au vin keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days, and tastes even better the next day. Reheat gently on the stove until bubbling, adding a splash of stock if the sauce is too thick. It freezes well for up to 3 months, thawed overnight in the fridge. Store the chicken in its sauce, so it does not dry out.

Ingredient notes

  • Bone in chicken stays juicy through a long braise. Boneless chicken cooks faster and can dry out, so reduce the oven time if you use it.
  • Use a wine you would drink. Cheap cooking wine makes a harsh sauce, while a fruity red wine gives depth and colour.
  • Pearl onions are small and sweet. If you cannot find them, use shallots cut into quarters.
  • Butter whisked in at the end gives the sauce a soft shine and rounds off the wine. Do not boil the sauce after adding it.

Variations

  • White wine version: use dry white wine and add cream at the end, for a lighter coq au vin blanc.
  • With potatoes: add small new potatoes to the pot for the last 40 minutes, so they cook in the sauce.
  • Slow cooker version: brown everything first, then cook on low for 5 to 6 hours, and reduce the sauce on the stove at the end.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the browning. The sauce will lack colour and depth, so take the time to brown in batches.
  • Boiling the braise hard. The chicken toughens and the sauce reduces too fast, so keep a gentle simmer and a low oven.
  • Using too much wine and no stock. The sauce turns sharp, so balance wine with stock and taste before serving.
  • Adding the butter too early. It splits in a long simmer, so whisk it in at the very end, off the boil.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make coq au vin without alcohol?

Yes, replace the wine with extra stock plus 2 tbsp of red wine vinegar and a little grape juice. The flavour is different, but the braise still works.

Can I use chicken breast?

You can, but add it for only the last 30 minutes, or it will dry out. Thighs and legs are much better for this dish.

What wine should I use?

A dry, fruity red wine, ideally from Burgundy. Avoid very tannic or oaky wines, which can turn bitter in a long braise.

Can I make it ahead?

Yes, and it improves. Cook it fully, cool, refrigerate, and reheat gently the next day.
