Ramen

45 min · 4 people · World Food · Japan

Ramen is comfort in a bowl, and a good home version does not need a broth that simmers all day. This chicken ramen builds flavour quickly, with garlic, ginger and spring onions fried in sesame oil, chicken stock enriched with soy and miso, and noodles cooked separately so the broth stays clear and the noodles stay springy. The egg is the finishing touch, soft in the middle and seasoned by the broth as you eat. Set out the toppings in small bowls and let everyone build their own bowl, hot broth last, so the noodles do not sit and soften. It is fast, warming and deeply satisfying, and it tastes like care, not like a shortcut.

Ingredients

  • Chicken breasts or thighs 500g
  • Chicken stock 1.5 litres
  • Ramen noodles 4 portions
  • Garlic 4 cloves, ginger 3cm piece
  • Soy sauce 3 tbsp, miso paste 2 tbsp
  • Sesame oil 1 tbsp, spring onions 4
  • Eggs 4, soft boiled
  • Chilli oil, sesame seeds and nori to serve

Method

  1. Boil the eggs for 6 and a half minutes, cool in cold water, peel, and halve them just before serving.
  2. Heat sesame oil in a large pot, fry the garlic, ginger and the white parts of the spring onions for 2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. Pour in the chicken stock, add soy sauce, and simmer gently for 15 minutes so the flavours come together.
  4. Cook the chicken in the simmering broth for 12 to 15 minutes until cooked through, then lift it out, slice it, and keep it warm.
  5. Whisk the miso with a ladle of hot broth until smooth, then stir it back into the pot. Do not boil hard after adding miso.
  6. Cook the noodles separately according to the packet, drain well, and divide between 4 warm bowls.
  7. Ladle the hot broth over the noodles, top with sliced chicken, egg halves, spring onion greens and sesame seeds.
  8. Serve at once with chilli oil and nori on the side, and eat while the broth is steaming hot.

Tip

Cook the noodles separately. Noodles cooked in the broth soak it up, turn the broth cloudy, and go soft before you serve.

Serving

Serves 4, in deep bowls, with chopsticks and a soup spoon, and the toppings in the middle of the table. Serve the broth steaming hot, with extra chilli oil, soy sauce and sesame seeds to hand, so each person can season their own bowl. Ramen is eaten at once, while the noodles are springy and the egg is still warm, so call everyone to the table before you ladle the broth.

Storage

Broth keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days, and freezes well for up to 1 month. Noodles and toppings do not keep well once combined, so store them separately and assemble fresh. Reheat the broth until boiling, cook fresh noodles, and add the toppings just before serving. Leftover sliced chicken keeps for 2 days in the fridge.

Variations

Common mistakes

['Boiling the broth hard after adding miso. It dulls the flavour and can split, so keep it at a gentle simmer.', 'Overcooking the noodles. They soften in the hot broth, so cook them just until springy and drain well.', 'Adding the egg too early. It overcooks in the hot broth, so add the halved egg at the very end.', 'Serving in cold bowls. The broth cools too fast, so warm the bowls with hot water first.']

Frequently asked questions

Can I use instant noodles?

Yes, but discard the flavour sachet and use this broth instead. Cook the noodle block separately and drain it well.

Can I make the broth ahead?

Yes. Make it up to 3 days ahead and refrigerate. Reheat until boiling, then add miso fresh and assemble the bowls.

How do I get a jammy egg?

Boil a fridge cold egg for 6 and a half minutes, cool at once in cold water, and peel gently. The yolk should be soft and just set.

Is ramen very salty?

It can be. Taste before adding extra soy sauce, because stock, soy and miso all add salt.
