Pozole

150 min · 6 people · World Food · Mexico

Published on 28 October 2026

Pozole is a celebration soup from Mexico, made with hominy, the large white corn kernels that puff and soften in a long simmer, and pork that cooks until it shreds with a spoon. The red version gets its colour and depth from dried guajillo and ancho chillies, blended smooth and stirred into the broth, while the toppings stay fresh and crisp, shredded cabbage, radish, onion, oregano and lime. It takes time, but most of that time is unattended, and the pot feeds a crowd. Cook it on a weekend, keep the broth rich and the toppings separate, and let everyone finish their own bowl at the table.

Ingredients

  • Pork shoulder 1kg (cut into large chunks)
  • White hominy 800g
  • drained (rinsed)
  • Dried guajillo chillies 4
  • ancho chillies 2
  • Onion 1
  • garlic 6 cloves
  • Bay leaves 2
  • oregano 2 tsp
  • cumin 1 tsp
  • Salt
  • water or light stock 3 litres
  • Shredded cabbage
  • radish
  • onion
  • lime to serve
  • Dried oregano
  • chilli flakes to serve

Method

  1. Soak the dried chillies in hot water for 15 minutes, drain, and blend with garlic and a little soaking water until smooth.
  2. Put the pork, hominy, onion, bay leaves and water in a large pot, bring to a boil, and skim off any foam.
  3. Reduce to a gentle simmer, cover partly, and cook for 90 minutes, topping up with hot water if the level falls too low.
  4. Strain the blended chilli sauce through a sieve into the pot, stir well, and season with salt, cumin and oregano.
  5. Simmer for 30 more minutes until the pork is tender enough to shred and the broth is rich and red.
  6. Lift out the pork, shred it with two forks, and return it to the pot. Taste and adjust the salt and chilli.
  7. Prepare the toppings while the pozole finishes, shredded cabbage, thin radish slices, diced onion, lime wedges and oregano.
  8. Ladle into deep bowls, add a generous amount of toppings, and serve with warm tortillas on the side.

Chef's tip

Strain the chilli sauce before adding it. The sieve catches skins and seeds, and the broth stays smooth and silky instead of gritty.

Serving

Serves 6, in deep bowls, with the toppings in separate bowls in the middle of the table. Each person adds cabbage for crunch, radish for bite, onion, oregano and a good squeeze of lime, which lifts the rich broth. Serve with warm corn tortillas for scooping, and keep extra chilli flakes on the side for those who want more heat.

Storage and reheating

Pozole keeps in the fridge for up to 4 days, and the flavour improves overnight. Store the broth and pork together, and the toppings separately. Reheat gently until boiling, adding a splash of water if it has thickened. It freezes well for up to 3 months, thawed overnight in the fridge. Do not freeze the fresh toppings.

Ingredient notes

  • Hominy is corn treated with lime, which makes the kernels large, soft and easy to digest. Canned hominy is ready to use, dried hominy needs overnight soaking and longer cooking.
  • Guajillo chillies give colour and a mild fruity heat. Ancho adds a darker, raisin like depth. Together they make the classic red broth.
  • Pork shoulder has enough fat to stay juicy through a long simmer. Lean pork dries out, so keep the chunks large and the simmer gentle.
  • Skim the foam at the start. It keeps the broth clear and the flavour clean.

Variations

  • Green pozole: replace the red chillies with tomatillos, green chillies and coriander, blended and added in the same way.
  • Chicken pozole: use bone in chicken thighs instead of pork, and reduce the simmering time to about 45 minutes.
  • Vegetarian pozole: use mushrooms and beans with vegetable stock, and add the same chilli sauce and toppings.

Common mistakes

  • Boiling hard the whole time. The pork toughens and the broth turns cloudy, so keep a gentle simmer.
  • Adding the chilli sauce without straining. Skins and seeds make the broth gritty.
  • Serving without fresh toppings. They are not decoration, they balance the rich broth with crunch and acid.
  • Under salting the broth. Taste at the end, because hominy and pork need a good amount of salt to shine.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a pressure cooker?

Yes. Cook the pork and hominy at pressure for 35 to 40 minutes, then finish with the chilli sauce in an open pot for 10 minutes.

Is pozole very spicy?

The red version is warm, not fiercely hot. Remove chilli seeds for a milder broth, and let chilli flakes at the table add heat.

Can I use dried hominy?

Yes, but soak it overnight and simmer it until tender before adding the pork, which can take an extra hour.

What do I serve with pozole?

Warm corn tortillas, and the fresh toppings listed here. A simple salad is not needed, the toppings are the salad.
